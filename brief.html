Best path on the model, and why it changes

On the baseline the model ranks sell and reinvest first, after tax. In 24 of the 162 what-if cases another path ranks first, so the ranking is not settled (site: What-if controls). If the basis is not reset at death, selling loses its advantage over renting (Q01).

For the estate attorney

  1. Which trust holds Property A, and is it in the gross estate? (Q01: the basis reset depends on it.)
  2. Does the trust plan to distribute Property A in kind, or sell it first? Who is the successor trustee?
  3. Is there a deceased spouse whose unused exclusion could be ported? Was a Form 706 filed? (Q02)
  4. Confirm the 60-day notice and the 150-day change-in-ownership filing dates for this trust.
  5. Confirm the Transferor is the Inheritor's parent (or meets the grandchild rule) for the parent-child exclusion. (Q06)

For the CPA / tax adviser

  1. Rerun the comparison with the Inheritor's actual bracket and filing status. (Q05)
  2. Confirm the basis step-up under the trust type, and the date-of-death appraisal used for basis. (Q01)
  3. Property B: the owner's basis and depreciation already claimed on the rented units. (Q03)
  4. Form 1041 for the trust or estate while the home is held; treatment of rent received before distribution.
  5. Whether the solar credit and the rent on Property B are reported as the model assumes. Unverified items are on Flagged for Review.

Deadlines (from the date of death)

Notice to beneficiaries within 60 days (Prob. Code 16061.7); change-in-ownership filing within 150 days (R&T Code 480); Form 706 within 9 months, six more with Form 4768; homeowners' exemption and principal-residence move within 1 year and the exclusion claim (BOE-19-P) within 3 years, only if the Inheritor moves in.
