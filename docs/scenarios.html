Qualitative factors not in the numbers

  • Housing quality: S1/S2 give the Heir a large Cupertino house (market rent about $134,460/yr in 2045); S3/S4 a 2-bedroom flat; S5/S6 a rental chosen by the Heir ($4,500/month in 2026 dollars assumed).
  • Concentration risk: keeping both properties leaves roughly $10 million in two Bay Area parcels; selling diversifies. FHFA shows both metros flat-to-down over the year to 2026Q2 and well below national appreciation over five years.
  • Landlord obligations: San Francisco rent control, just-cause eviction, relocation payments and (if revived) the Empty Homes Tax add cost and friction to S1/S3/S4/S5. Professional management (6%) is assumed.
  • Liquidity and simplicity: S6 is the simplest and most liquid; S4 keeps one building to manage.
  • Legal risk: Prop 19 could be loosened (initiatives have failed so far) or the federal exclusion lowered by a future Congress — neither changes the ranking much because the drivers are carrying cost vs. investment return.
