Finding 1

Federal estate tax will not apply to most families — the exemption is $15,000,000 per person and now permanent

The “considerable amount of money” in the trust is only taxable federally if the decedent’s total estate (everything owned + prior taxable gifts) exceeds $15M per person / $30M married in 2026, indexed for inflation after 2026. There is no California estate, inheritance, or gift tax. A total estate of, say, $8M — even $14M — owes $0 federal estate tax.

Implication: Unless the trust + all other assets + prior gifts exceed $15M (or $30M married), you inherit tax-free at transfer. What you pay later is property tax, income tax on rentals/IRAs, and capital gains when you sell — not an “inheritance tax.” Full analysis: Tax Laws.

Finding 2

Your property tax will jump 3–5× the day you inherit — that’s the biggest recurring cost

Prop 13 kept the parents’ assessed values ultra-low ($550,540 Cupertino; $114,250 SF). At inheritance, the county reassesses unless you qualify for Prop 19’s narrow live-in exclusion. Even with that exclusion, Cupertino’s bill roughly triples; without it, quadruples.

Finding 3

Prop 19’s $1,044,586 exclusion saves ~$12,330/yr — but only if one heir moves in within 1 year and files

  • ✔ Property was parents’ principal residence at transfer
  • ✔ Child makes it their principal residence within 1 year of death/transfer
  • ✔ Child files Homeowners’ Exemption (BOE-266) within 1 year
  • ✔ Child files BOE-19-P exclusion claim within 3 years (or before sale)
  • ✔ Continuous occupancy; if move out later, reassessed to FMV-at-transfer + inflation

Rental, vacation, or investment property = no exclusion. Full market reassessment. One sibling occupying satisfies for that interest; others’ shares reassess. BOE Prop 19 FAQ

Savings = $1,044,586 cap × 1.18% = $12,326 ≈ $12,330/yr. Compounds at 2%/yr thereafter.

Finding 4

Stepped-up basis wipes out decades of capital gains — sell quickly and owe nearly $0 income tax

Under IRC §1014, your cost basis becomes fair market value at death. If Cupertino is worth $3.1M when inherited (and parents’ basis was far lower), the ~$2M+ of appreciation during their lifetime is permanently erased for income tax. Sell for $3.1M the next month → ~$0 federal/CA capital gains. Gain after death is taxed at 0%/15%/20% federal + 3.8% NIIT (> $200k single / $250k joint) + CA 1–13.3% (CA taxes gains as ordinary income).

Primary residence exclusion ($250k single / $500k joint) under IRC §121 only helps if you lived there 2 of last 5 years — it does not stack with stepped-up basis when selling immediately. Depreciation recapture not applicable to personal residence. Rental depreciation recapture (§1250, max 25%) would apply to the SF rental portion if sold.

By 2050, even the “discounted” live-in tax exceeds $92k/yr. That is why many families who intend to inherit in the 2040s decide now whether they will actually occupy the house — or plan to sell within 1–2 years of inheritance and redeploy the equity.

Law-change exposure: Prop 13’s 1% + 2% is constitutional (Art. XIIIA) and unlikely to be repealed, but reassessment exclusions (Prop 19) could be tightened or loosened. A 2024 initiative to restore the old $1M + residence exclusion failed to qualify. At federal level, the $15M exemption is now permanent under OBBBA — no 2026 “sunset” cut to ~$7M. Watch for proposals to limit stepped-up basis or raise NIIT, but nothing enacted as of Nov 2026. See Tax Laws → Potential Changes.

📌 Decision Recommendation — Based on Current Law & Numbers Above

Maximize profit · Minimize cost · Reduce uncertainty

If you want to maximize wealth and flexibility:

  1. Keep the SF Outer Sunset house — live in its 3rd unit, retain $85,800 gross rent, low current tax ($2,440). It’s your best rental asset.
  2. For Cupertino: plan to sell within 12 months of inheritance unless you genuinely want to live there. Stepped-up basis makes the sale ~tax-free; reinvest net ~$2.93M into broad market (see wealth comparison).
  3. If you DO want to live in Cupertino — move in within 1 year, file BOE-266 + BOE-19-P immediately to lock the $12,330/yr Prop 19 saving. Budget for $24k+ property tax (2026 dollars) growing 2%/yr.

If you want to keep Cupertino as a legacy home:

  1. Accept $36,580/yr (or $24,250/yr if you live there) in property tax in 2026 dollars, plus ~$7,320/yr insurance/water/electric/maintenance — much of any rent is consumed.
  2. Run the 20-year projection: at 3.8% rent growth vs 2% tax growth, it eventually cash-flows better — but you carry concentration risk (single zip, single asset) and earthquake/wildfire exposure.
  3. Consider a 1031 exchange alternative if you inherit and want real estate exposure without living there (defers gain, but resets property tax anyway).
