Cupertino & San Francisco
Inherited-Property Decision Analysis

A complete, source-verified analysis of whether to live in, rent out, or sell a Cupertino home inherited through a trust around 2040–2050, given a second, rent-controlled San Francisco property that will also pass to the same heirs.

Bottom line up front. On every assumption set we tested, selling Property A at the time of inheritance and reinvesting the proceeds produces the highest after-tax wealth — by a wide margin. Holding the house only becomes competitive if Cupertino home prices compound above 9.5% per year for 25 years (each of the last two five-year periods delivered roughly 5.0%–6.5%). The reason is arithmetic, not opinion: at the transfer year the house returns about 2.7% a year on its market value — the 4.0% appreciation less a 1.34% negative operating yield from property tax and carrying costs — while the same capital in a diversified portfolio is modeled at 6.5% net after tax drag.

Read this before relying on the sale numbers. The sale case wins partly because the analysis re-levers: the proceeds are invested at a modeled 7.0% gross return. If the family would instead spend the proceeds, or hold them in cash, the ranking changes completely. The sale result assumes disciplined reinvestment, which is a behavioural assumption, not a market fact.
