4. What to monitor, and when

  1. Every February in an odd-numbered year — the BOE's indexed Proposition 19 cap. Track it at boe.ca.gov Letters to Assessors.
  2. Every January — the BOE's California CPI factor for the coming assessment roll, which sets the 2% growth cap's actual value for the year.
  3. Every November–December — the SF Rent Board's allowable increase for the year beginning 1 March.
  4. Continuously — Revenue and Taxation Code §13301 and any constitutional amendment proposals touching estate or wealth taxation.
  5. Continuously — Proposition 13 and Proposition 19 amendment proposals. These move rarely but would be decisive.

5. Net effect of law changes on the recommendation

No enacted or pending change overturns the recommendation. The direction of enacted change since 2021 has been consistently against holding inherited property cheaply: Proposition 19 removed the rental exclusion, and just-cause and rent-cap rules have tightened. The estate tax picture improved, but estate tax was never the binding constraint here. If anything, the legislative trend strengthens the case for selling Property A.

The one change that would flip the answer is restoration of a broad parent-child exclusion that applies to rental property — that would let Property B keep its near-zero basis, and could make the rental case for Property A viable too. No such measure is pending. Do not plan around it.
