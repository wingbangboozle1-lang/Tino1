Inheritance & Estate Tax

The short answer is that there is no inheritance tax on this estate, at either the federal or the state level, on any realistic set of values through 2050. That is a matter of statute, not estimation. The long answer is that the federal exclusion is scheduled to be indexed for inflation and can be changed by Congress, so the margin should be measured rather than assumed.

1. California: no inheritance tax, by statute

This is not a policy gap or an oversight. California affirmatively and permanently prohibits the tax, in the starkest language available:

"Neither the state nor any political subdivision of the state shall impose any gift, inheritance, succession, legacy, income, or estate tax, or any other tax, on gifts or on the estate or inheritance of any person or on or by reason of any transfer occurring by reason of a death."
— Cal. Rev. & Tax. Code §13301 R&TC §13301 CA Law

California has no inheritance tax because voters eliminated it in 1982 (Proposition 6), and the prohibition is part of the Revenue and Taxation Code. Any future California inheritance or wealth tax would require a constitutional amendment and a statewide vote. See Law Watch for the proposals that have been attempted.

The 2026 jump from $13,990,000 to $15,000,000 was legislated, and it is permanent. Public Law 119-21 (the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, enacted 4 July 2025) set the basic exclusion at $15,000,000 for decedents dying after 31 December 2025 and removed the scheduled 2026 sunset that would otherwise have cut it roughly in half. The IRS confirmed the figure in Revenue Procedure 2025-32. The amount is indexed for inflation after 2026. IRS IR-2025-103 IRS

Portability is not automatic. A surviving spouse can only use a deceased spouse's unused exclusion if the estate files a timely Form 706 electing portability — even where no tax is owed and no return would otherwise be required. Families routinely miss this, and it is worth up to $15,000,000 of additional exclusion. Flag the Form 706 deadline when the first spouse dies.

Where it could become a problem. At 4.0%/yr the real-estate component alone crosses the single-person $15,000,000 exclusion between 2060 and 2065 — about $18.6 million by 2060 against the $15 million threshold. A 2045–2050 transfer is comfortably inside it. But the estate also includes "a considerable amount of money." If the cash and securities total more than roughly $5,600,000 at the time of death in 2045, a single decedent's estate would exceed $15,000,000 and would owe federal estate tax at 40% on the excess. That threshold is the number to watch. For a married couple with a properly elected portability, the equivalent figure is about $20,600,000. Derived

4. The step-up in basis — the larger tax benefit

For most families in this position the estate tax is irrelevant and the basis step-up is the real money. When a beneficiary inherits property, its income-tax basis becomes the fair market value on the date of death.

"Generally, the basis of property inherited from a decedent is… the FMV of the property at the date of the individual's death." IRS Pub 551 IRS

All of the appreciation that occurred during the parent's lifetime is never taxed. If Property A is worth $3,100,000 today against a basis that may be only a few hundred thousand dollars, the entire gain escapes income tax at death.

Do not deed the houses to the children before death. A lifetime gift carries over the parent's low basis instead of stepping it up, and because the properties are in a trust the transfer can be arranged at death with no probate. Gifting them early would very likely convert a $0 tax into a six-figure one. The one exception is if the estate is large enough to face a 40% estate tax — which, on these numbers, it is not.

Watch the retirement accounts. These are frequently the largest single tax exposure in an inheritance and they are routinely overlooked because they are not "real estate." A distribution from an inherited traditional IRA is ordinary income, and because the estate or trust brackets compress, it can be taxed at the top rate on a relatively modest amount. Model the distribution schedule before the estate is funded.

6. Conclusion on inheritance tax

Conclusion

No inheritance tax applies. Plan around income tax instead.

California imposes none by statute. The federal exclusion of $15,000,000 per person ($30,000,000 for a properly-elected couple) comfortably exceeds this estate's real-estate component through 2050. The tax work that actually matters here is preserving the basis step-up by letting the property pass at death rather than by gift, and managing the timing of retirement account distributions.

The one number to track: total estate value over $15,000,000 for a single decedent, or $30,000,000 for a surviving couple.
