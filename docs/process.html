The Inheritance Process, Step by Step

What actually happens, in order, from a death to a recorded deed — with the deadline attached to each step. Because both properties are held in a trust, this is a trust administration, not a probate, which removes most of the cost and delay.

The trust is the single biggest advantage here. A probate in Santa Clara or San Francisco County routinely takes 12–24 months and costs the estate a statutory percentage of gross value — several tens of thousands of dollars on properties of this size. Held in a properly funded trust, the same transfer can be completed in weeks with no court involvement. Confirm the properties are actually titled in the trust; an unfunded trust delivers none of this.

Phase 1 — Immediately at death (days 0–30)

  1. Obtain certified death certificates. Order 10–15 originals. Nearly every institution in this list will require one, and they are not readily reusable. Ordering more later adds weeks.
  2. Locate the original trust instrument and any amendments. The trustee needs the signed original, not a copy. Confirm which version is operative — later amendments supersede earlier ones, and a restatement replaces the whole document.
  3. Confirm successor trustee. The trust names who takes over. If the successor is unable or unwilling, the document specifies the next in line. A trustee's acceptance is often required in writing by banks and title companies.
  4. Secure both properties. Change locks if needed, collect keys, redirect mail, and confirm utilities stay on. Tell the insurer the property is now vacant or trustee-owned — many policies void coverage if occupancy changes without notice.
  5. Obtain an EIN for the trust from the IRS, using Form SS-4. Free and immediate online. This is required before the trust can open a bank account or file a return.
  6. Notify Social Security and stop or redirect income. Also notify the mortgage servicer if either property has a loan.

Phase 2 — Establish authority (days 15–60)

  1. Trustee takes control of assets. Using the trust instrument, the death certificate, and the EIN, the successor trustee re-titles accounts and asserts authority over the properties. Banks and brokerages have their own forms; expect each to differ.
  2. Date-of-death valuation of both properties. This is the most important single number in the whole process, because it sets the new income-tax basis. Get a written appraisal effective the date of death from a licensed appraiser — not an estimate, and not the county's assessed value, which is a different concept entirely. See Inheritance for why the basis matters so much.
  3. Inventory all assets. Real property, bank and brokerage accounts, retirement accounts, life insurance, vehicles, personal property. Retirement accounts and life insurance pass by beneficiary designation, not through the trust — they follow the form on file at the institution.
  4. File Form 56 with the IRS to notify of the fiduciary relationship. Optional but prudent; it ensures the IRS writes to the trustee rather than the decedent.

Phase 3 — Property tax filings (the hard deadlines)

These carry real money and are where families most often lose it. All three clocks start at death.

  1. File the Proposition 19 claim, form BOE-19-P, with the county assessor. Deadline: 3 years from the date of transfer or before sale to a third party, whichever is first
    This is the claim that preserves the parent's low assessed value on Property A. Without it the assessor will reassess to market. It is not automatic. BOE Prop 19 BOE
  2. File the homeowners' or disabled veterans' exemption claim. Deadline: 1 year from the date of transfer
    Separate from BOE-19-P and equally mandatory. Failing this forfeits the Proposition 19 exclusion entirely, even if BOE-19-P was filed on time. The Board of Equalization is explicit that the exclusion applies only if the heir applies for the exemption within one year. BOE LTA 2026/026 BOE
  3. Actually occupy Property A within one year. Deadline: 1 year from the date of transfer
    Not a filing — a physical requirement. There are "no exceptions to this rule." If no heir moves in, Property A is reassessed to full market value and the exemption is moot. BOE LTA 2026/026 BOE
  4. Notify the assessor of the change in ownership for Property B. Deadline: as required by the county
    Property B is a rental and does not qualify for the exclusion. It will be reassessed, and a supplemental assessment will issue for the difference between the old and new values, prorated from the transfer date. Budget for it. SF Treasurer SF

The 1-year and 3-year clocks are different, and missing the shorter one is fatal. These are routinely confused. BOE-19-P gets three years. The homeowners' exemption gets one. If the exemption is missed, the three-year BOE-19-P window is worthless. Put both dates on a calendar the day the death certificate arrives.

Phase 4 — Income tax filings (by 15 April of the following year)

  1. File the decedent's final Form 1040 and California Form 540. Covers income from 1 January through the date of death. Also file any prior-year returns not yet filed.
  2. Determine whether a federal Form 706 is required. Likely not, on these values
    The gross estate here is far below $15,000,000. But if a spouse survives, file Form 706 anyway to elect portability — it preserves the deceased spouse's unused exclusion for the survivor, and it is the only way to get it. This applies even where no tax is due and no return would otherwise be required. IRS Estate Tax IRS
  3. File the trust's Form 1041 and California Form 541 for each year the trust is open. Note that a trust reaches the top federal rate at roughly $16,100 of income in 2026 — compressed brackets, so distributions should be planned rather than improvised.
  4. Plan inherited retirement account distributions. A non-spouse beneficiary generally must empty the account within 10 years. Those distributions are ordinary income. This is frequently the largest tax item in an inheritance and is best modelled before the estate is distributed.

Phase 5 — Transfer title and decide (months 2–12)

  1. Record the deed transferring each property from the trust to the beneficiaries. An assessor's parcel number change follows. This does not trigger a documentary transfer tax when it is a distribution from a trust to a beneficiary, but confirm with the county recorder.
  2. Notify insurers. Insurance must be rewritten in the name of the trust or the beneficiaries. Vacancy exclusions bite here — a vacant home is often uninsured after 30–60 days unless a vacancy endorsement is purchased.
  3. Make the occupation decision for Property A, on a timetable. The decision must be made before the 1-year mark, because the exclusion is binary: occupy or be reassessed. Run the analysis on Scenarios before that date, not after it.
  4. If selling, plan the timing. Federal Section 121 exclusion of $500,000 requires two of the last five years of ownership and use as a principal residence. If an heir occupies the property for at least two years after inheritance, a later sale can use the exclusion. If sold immediately after inheritance, no exclusion is needed because the gain is zero. IRS Topic 701 IRS
  5. If renting, reset every operating assumption. Landlord policy instead of homeowners, new rent roll, tenant screening, and a capital reserve. The rental scenario is on Carrying Costs.

Three things this page cannot tell you. Whether the trust is properly funded; whether the trust's terms distribute the properties outright or hold them in further trust; and whether the trust is a grantor trust, a bypass trust, or something else with different income-tax consequences. All three change the picture materially. Review the actual instrument with a California attorney before acting on any of it.
