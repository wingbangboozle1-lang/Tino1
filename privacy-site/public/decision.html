Provisional plan, not a verified optimum

Retain the existing home as the baseline. At inheritance, compare a prompt sale against a documented rental offer and a genuine desire to occupy A. Favor sale/diversification if net rent and realistic appreciation cannot compensate for taxes, repairs, illiquidity and concentration. Favor keeping A only if it clears that hurdle or provides valued housing benefits. No household winner can be honestly established with the missing facts.

A numerical teaching example

Invented, unrelated home: $1,000,000 value and inherited basis; no mortgage. Immediate sale cost 5%, giving $950,000 to invest. Effective portfolio return 5% annually after all investment taxes and fees, including liquidation is assumed, not promised. Rent starts at $36,000 per year; vacancy 5%; management 8% of collected rent. First-year property tax $12,000, insurance $3,000, water $2,000, electricity $1,000, repairs $6,000 and capital work $4,000. Rent and non-tax costs rise 2.5%; assessed base rises 2%. These are invented assumptions, NOT official prices or regional estimates.

Why live-in has no fabricated ranking

Without an official valuation of alternative housing use or documented private preference, assigning a dollar benefit to moving would be invented. Live-in stays a separate decision gate. This illustrative comparison excludes B, estate-tax allocations, leverage and Section 121 benefits from both paths. No claim that these omissions are immaterial.
