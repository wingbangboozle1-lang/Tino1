REFERENCE / TERMS

Glossary

Plain-language definitions used throughout the guide. Definitions are general; the linked official context controls.

Terms and meanings · official context, not household verification
TermMeaning / limitationOfficial context
Adjusted basisTax basis used to calculate gain or loss; inherited-property basis requires review of the transfer and trust facts.IRS · Inherited property basis ↗
Factored base-year valueCalifornia property-tax value adjusted annually within statutory rules; distinct from income-tax basis.California · RTC §51 ↗
Family-home exclusionA limited California property-tax reassessment exclusion that depends on relationship, use, claims and current law.BOE · Publication 800-1 (Prop 19) ↗
Gross estateAssets included in the federal estate-tax calculation; not the same as what one beneficiary receives.IRS · Estate tax ↗
PortabilityPotential transfer of a deceased spouse’s unused federal exclusion, generally requiring a timely estate-tax return election.IRS · Estate tax FAQ ↗
Supplemental assessmentAn assessment that may follow a change in ownership or new construction; verify the local bill and applicable process.California BOE · Proposition 19 ↗
Rental income / expensesRent and qualifying expenses are reported under tax rules; gross rent is not after-tax profit.IRS · Topic 414 ↗
Sequence riskRisk that the timing/order of returns changes outcomes when there are withdrawals, taxes or expenses; not captured by a no-cash-flow terminal return alone.SEC · Asset allocation and diversification ↗
Treasury reinvestment riskThe future return on maturing short Treasury holdings is unknown; a model must not carry today’s yield forward as a fact.SEC · Asset allocation and diversification ↗
Synthetic scenarioA deliberately invented stress path used to test sensitivity; not a historical replay, forecast, or probability.Federal Reserve History · Great Recession ↗
A note on similar words

Property-tax assessed value, income-tax basis, fair market value and sale proceeds are different measures. Do not substitute one for another in an attorney or tax calculation.
