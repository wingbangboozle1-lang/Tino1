Transparent stress paths

Begin with unrelated investment capital of $1,000,000. Smooth reference: 5% effective net return, 2.5% inflation for 50 years. Crash: −40% in year 1, then 5%. Inflation shock: 3% return and 8% inflation for five years, then 5% / 2.5%. Stagnation: 0% for ten years, then 5%, with 2.5% inflation throughout. Late crash: 5% except −40% in year 20; inflation 2.5%. No contributions, withdrawals or separate taxes; returns are assumed net of all tax/fees. These are invented stresses, not historical replays, predictions or probabilities.

Why a single constant inflation rate is not a baseline: the official record

The table below is arithmetic on the published annual CPI-U changes from the official BLS table linked in every row. It is not a forecast, not a probability and not a real return. It exists for one purpose: to show how far one assumed rate can sit from observed experience, which is why this page stresses uneven inflation instead of projecting a smooth average.

Sequence-risk distinction

With no cash flows, the same returns in a different order have the same terminal product. Early and late crash paths therefore match at year 50. With tax payments, repairs or withdrawals, sequence matters: selling depressed assets can leave fewer assets to participate in recovery. Do not claim this lump-sum illustration measures withdrawal sequence risk.
