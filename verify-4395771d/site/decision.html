Live in it, rent it out, or sell and invest

Each choice is followed year by year after the inheritance, after tax. The heir lives in the San Francisco unit unless they move to Cupertino, so every choice includes a home.

What the numbers say

  • Sell and invest wins in the baseline because the proceeds grow at the assumed 7.0% while the home grows at 4.0% and, once reassessed, costs $77,610 a year in property tax. The sale itself is almost untaxed because the basis resets at death. IRS FAQ — Gifts & inheritances
  • Keep and rent out is the middle path. The home is taxed on its full market value (no relief for a rental), the rent roughly covers the costs, and the result rides on home prices. It beats selling only if stocks return less than about 4.7% a year. BOE Letter to Assessors 2026/026, Q2
  • Move in is the most expensive in money: the capital stays in the house and the running costs are paid from other income. Compared with selling, living there costs about $1,201,260 over the first 10 years and $3,281,430 over 20 years, in 2026 dollars. That is the price of the home as a place to live — a personal choice the model cannot make.
  • Sell and hold cash is the worst. If the proceeds would sit in savings, keep the house instead.
  • Moving in keeps the door open. A sale later is still possible; the first $250,000 of gain after the death is excluded for an owner who has lived there two years ($500,000 on a joint return). IRS Topic no. 701
