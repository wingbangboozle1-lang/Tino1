The short answer

  1. For the most money, sell the Cupertino home soon after inheriting it and invest the proceeds in a broad stock index fund. Its tax basis resets to its value on the date of death, so a prompt sale leaves little or no gain to tax. With an inheritance in 2045, this ends 20 years later about $5,487,450 ahead of keeping the home and renting it out, and about $8,595,960 ahead of moving in (after tax, in dollars of that year). Full comparison.
  2. That only holds if the money is really invested. Selling and leaving the proceeds in cash or savings is the weakest choice: it ends about $5,616,920 behind keeping the home and renting it out.
  3. If you want to live there, move in within one year and file two forms. That keeps part of the low assessed value and saves about $17,600 of property tax in the first year compared with not qualifying. There are no exceptions to the one-year deadline. Steps and deadlines.
  4. Keep the San Francisco building. Because the heir already owns it, the inheritance changes nothing about it: it is not reassessed (its tax stays near $2,470 a year; a buyer today would pay about $22,360), it is not part of the taxable estate, and it gets no reset of tax basis — so selling it would be taxed on its whole gain. It also gives the heir a home and rental income whichever choice is made for Cupertino.
  5. Estate tax is a federal question only. Only the Cupertino home and the other inherited assets count. For a death in 2045 the exclusion is projected near $24,143,520; after the home there would be room for about $17,612,280 of other assets before any tax, and roughly twice the exclusion if a spouse's unused amount was preserved. Thresholds by year.
  6. History agrees most of the time, not always. Replaying the real 1977–2025 Bay Area record from every starting year, selling and investing came out ahead after 20 years in 48 of 49 cases (after 10 years: 40 of 49). It lost when a stock crash came first, and in a Japan-style stagnation cash beat everything. Black swans.

All comparisons assume the heir lives in the San Francisco unit unless they move to Cupertino, and include an illustrative $1,000,000 (2026 dollars) of other inherited money in every choice. 6 inputs could not be confirmed and several are assumptions — see Flagged for Review.
