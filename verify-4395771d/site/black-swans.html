Black swans: the same decision under real economic history

Growth and inflation are never steady for 50 years. This page replaces the smooth baseline with what actually happened — year by year, in order — and asks whether the answer survives.

What the history says

  • Over 20 years or more, selling and investing in stocks won almost every time. Bay Area record 1977–2025: 48 of 49 histories. National record 1929–2025, which includes the Depression: 90 of 97.
  • Over 10 years it is closer. Keeping the house and renting it out won 7 of 49 Bay Area histories and 21 of 97 national ones — the cases where a stock crash came soon after the inheritance.
  • The worst outcomes for stocks are worse. After 10 years the poorest Bay Area history left the stock choice at $3,662,760 against $5,515,420 for keeping and renting (2026 dollars). Do not put money needed within a few years into shares.
  • In a Japan-style stagnation, cash won and both the house and the shares lost value for two decades. Across the eight episodes and three horizons below (24 cases), the 60/40 mix of shares and cash was the best choice in 1 and the worst in 0.
  • Moving in was never the best choice on money alone in any history tried. Its value is the home itself.

Practical reading: sell if the proceeds can stay invested for 20 years or more; hold part in cash or short bonds for what is needed sooner; do not sell into a housing slump if the home can be carried through it.
