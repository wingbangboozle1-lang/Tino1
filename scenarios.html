Live in it, rent it, or sell it

One inheritance, three legal paths, quantified after tax under seven economic regimes. Decision rule, in one line: compare what the net sale proceeds can earn outside the house against what the house itself earns inside, after both are taxed — with 2026’s step-up basis making the sale a tax-free starting gun.

2 · Assumptions that drive the answer (all visible, all labelled)

  • Inheritance year 2045 (range 2040–2050 doesn’t change the ranking — see Estate tax headroom table); horizon 2060.
  • A appreciation +3.5%/yr (anchor FHFA CA 5-yr +17.3%, 2026Q2; CA 1991–2026 ≈ +6.4%/yr long-run); B +3.0%/yr (SF metro −2.4% y/y currently; long-run positive).
  • Portfolio +7.0%/yr nominal gross (flagged: 2026 reality is 10-yr ≈ 4.9%, cash ≈ 3.75–4.00%; a 5% portfolio world is the “energyshock” regime row). Dividends/interest taxed annually? Ignored for clarity — terminal-tax accounting instead.
  • Terminal taxation (2060): capital-gains at ≈ 37.1% (fed 20% + 3.8% NIIT + CA 13.3% incl. 1% MHST) on post-step-up appreciation only; selling costs A 5.0% (agent ≈ 4.9% + SCC 0.11% transfer tax), B 5.75% (agent + SF 0.75% tier $1M–$10M).
  • Integer-invariant flows: B’s controlled rents +1.6%/yr; A rent (S3) +3.0%/yr from ≈ $6,390/mo 2026 benchmark; water/electric/insurance/maintenance as in Costs; B tax/assessed as computed in Projections.
  • Excluded (deliberately): ordinary income tax on rents & wages (applies equally to S2/S3), depreciation recapture nuance, mortgage effects (B is owned free-and-clear — assumed), estate tax (none expected — see Estate tax), transaction frictions of multiple moves.

4 · The honest counterweights (what the spreadsheet can’t price)

  • Housing consumption value: living in A (or B’s unit) is worth ≈ $6,400/mo (A) / ≈ $3,200/mo (B-pattern rent) in avoided rent — ≈ $1,150,000–$2,400,000 nominal over 15 years. This does not close an $8.6M gap to S1 on its own, but it is real utility, not zero.
  • Emotional / legacy value: staying in the home you grew up in has value no model prices. It costs ≈ $8.58M nominal (vs S1) — decide knowingly.
  • Cash-flow mechanics: S1 concentrates all cash into the investment account but forces discipline (no raid); S2/S3 keep a house and dribble cash (rent minus costs ≈ +$5,000–9,000/mo for A in S3 — thin, illiquid).
  • Liquidity of future estate: S1’s account pays Form 706 taxes/debts/costs without distress sales; S2/S3 may force a distressed sale later anyway (or a §6166 installment fight).
  • Stagflation flip is the outlier scenario that matters: if you assign it > ≈ 40% probability, holding real assets (S3) becomes defensible; if you see 2026’s CPI 3.7% as sticky-but-mean-reverting (our baseline), S1 dominates. Either way, S2 is never the winner — it is strictly worse than S3 on both housing cost and tax.

5 · Decision matrix — apply in order

  1. Does the heir want to live in A for the long haul? If yes → S2 is available; still run the numbers (bill $53,320/yr vs selling) — many “I want to live there” plans die on the tax bill alone. Note Prop 19 clock (12 months!).
  2. Does the estate need cash or diversification? (other assets > $14,000,000, debts, liquidity shortfall, estate > exclusion) → sell; A is the only liquid slice without uprooting tenancies.
  3. Is CPI ≥ 5% y/y with equities negative in real terms, two quarters running? → reconsider holding A (S3) — the only regime that flips this model. Recheck quarterly while it lasts.
  4. Otherwise → S1. Sell promptly, lock the step-up, reinvest diversified, ladder into the market over 6–12 months (don’t lump-sum into a -7.5%-per-year GFC if you inherit into one).
  5. Whatever you choose for A, keep B — the analysis never supports selling B before A; its yield, controlled rents and heir-occupancy option dominate alternatives.

Bottom line. Inherit → (if you want A as a home: move in within 12 months, BOE-19-P within 3 years — be honest that it costs ≈ $8.6M) → otherwise sell at the step-up, invest diversified, live in B’s third unit, keep B forever. Re-evaluate only if the stagflation tripwires fire. This is the profit-maximising answer; every alternative is money for sentiment.
