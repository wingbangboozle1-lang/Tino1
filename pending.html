Law changes: enacted, pending, and due to move

What California and federal law now say, what was decided at the ballot ten days ago, and what is scheduled to change before a 2040 to 2050 transfer. Every row links the official page it was read on; unverified items are left blank and flagged.

Why this page exists. The decision is being made now for a transfer expected between 2040 and 2050. Almost every rule that decides the answer can change in that window, and several of them changed in the twelve months before this page was written. Each row below was read on an official page; where an official page does not exist, the figure is left blank and flagged rather than guessed. Nothing on this page is advice, and nothing here changes the baseline numbers.

Reading the certainty of a row: "in force" means the text was read on the statute or agency page today; "unofficial returns" means a count that can still move until 2026-12-11; "not enacted" means a proposal; "left blank" means no official figure exists yet.

SB 293 (2025) rewrote the Proposition 19 claim clock for fire-damaged parcelsIn force since 2026-01-01. Where the assessor reassesses a property because it was damaged or destroyed by the 2025 Palisades, Eaton, Hurst, Lidia, Sunset or Woodley fire and a change in ownership had gone unrecorded, a claim is timely for three years after the notice of supplemental or escape assessment, and the homeowners' exemption filing is timely within a year of that notice. Only for claims filed before 2031-01-01.A 2040 to 2050 transfer is outside the relief, so the general rule governs: three years from the transfer, or before the home is sold or the Inheritor stops living in it, whichever comes first; six months from a supplemental notice also counts as timely.
