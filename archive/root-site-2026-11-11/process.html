How the inheritance actually happens

Fourteen steps from death to settled ownership, in calendar order, with every deadline, form number and statute. [Transferor] → revocable trust → [Heir]. No probate if the trust is funded. Work with a California estate attorney for execution; this page is the map, not the signature.

Phase 0 — while the transferor is alive (now–2045)

  1. Verify the trust is funded: certified vesting deeds show “[Transferor], as Trustee of the [Family] Revocable Trust” as owner of [Property A]. If A is still in the transferor’s individual name → the trust never owned it → probate risk and NO step-up preservation game certainty; fix with a funding deed now. Confirm the Homeowners’ Exemption sits on one property only (A if it’s the transferor’s residence). R&TC §480 · BOE Prop 19
  2. Collect the paper: trust (all amendments), latest property-tax bills (A & B), insurance declarations, solar PPA/lease or warranties, rental agreements (B), plus a sealed letter of instruction. Store originals with the attorney; keep copies accessible.
  3. Decide the A-or-B question early (privately): if the transferor’s principal residence is A, Prop 19’s exclusion runs on A. If actually B (a unit thereof), exclusion runs on that unit instead — and A is fully reassessed at death regardless of the heir’s plans. One fact changes four figures on every other page of this site.
  4. Pre-select appraisers: for date-of-death FMV of A and B. A Certified Residential Appraiser (or two, for a contested estate) — required for basis, Prop 19 worksheet, and any dispute.

Phase 1 — the first 60 days

  1. Death certificate — order 8–10 certified copies.
  2. Trustee’s duty of notice (PC §16061.7) — within 60 days of death, the trustee must send a notarized notification (with a copy of the trust terms affecting the recipient) to every beneficiary and every heir-at-law, including disinherited ones. This notice starts the 120-day window for trust contests. Failure: damages + suspended 120-day clock. Prob. Code §16061.7 · CA Courts
  3. Inventory — list all trust assets/liabilities with values (bank, brokerage, retirement, both properties, vehicles, debts); beneficiaries receive an accounting on request (PC §16062).
  4. Notify the assessors — BOE-502-D (Death of Real Property Owner) to Santa Clara County and San Francisco County within 150 days of death (R&TC §480). Late or omitted filing: penalty = greater of $100 or 10% of the new tax. This is the form that triggers reassessment processing — it does not itself compute your bill. R&TC §480 · BOE property-tax

Phase 2 — the appraisal & the tax filings (days 60–365)

  1. Date-of-death appraisals — both properties. IRS: qualified appraisal for the estate; county: FBYV/Prop 19 worksheet; basis: new carryover value = FMV at death (plus any post-death additions at cost). IRS Pub. 551 · BOE LTA 2026/026
  2. Federal Form 706 — due 9 months after death (payment same day). Required only if gross estate + prior taxable gifts > $15,000,000 (2026; indexed). File it anyway if the transferor was married to elect portability — an estate not required to file can elect within 5 years of death (Rev. Proc. 2022-32). CA has no estate tax, no return. Form 706 instructions · Rev. Proc. 2022-32
  3. Property-tax decisions (choose your clock) — if moving into A: file BOE-266 (Homeowners’ Exemption) within 1 year of the date-of-death transfer; then file BOE-19-P to claim the parent–child exclusion within 3 years (late = prospective relief only). B gets no exclusion — expect full reassessment (≈ $39,050/yr 2045) whether it’s rented or kept. BOE Prop 19 — claim forms · LTA 2026/026
  4. Record the change in ownership — trustee’s deed from trustee to (new) trustee(s)/heirs recorded with the county; title company or attorney prepares it; update deed, insurer, and (if B) the Rent Board / tenant notices for owner change (informational only — tenancies unaffected).

Phase 3 — settle & harden (year 1–3)

  1. Re-budget the property tax reality — A: $10,780 (2044) → $53,320 (live-in) or $71,030 (no live-in); B: $3,670 → $39,050. Confirm bills with each county (SCC online/DTAC · SF Treasurer/Office of the Treasurer & Tax Collector). Check whether Prop 8 decline-in-value or direct-charge errors apply — read the itemised bill, not just the total. SCC DTAC · SF Treasurer
  2. Harden the new structure — update/assign insurance (trustee as named insured; landlord policy for B; umbrella), solar PPA assignment, B’s leases, and calendar: annual premium review (growing ≈ 5%/yr), annual rent increase notices (≤ 1.6% 2026–27 if rent-controlled), Prop 19 annual compliance if any A-exclusion conditions change (heir must continue to occupy A as principal residence). Keep every record 7+ years; store BOE-19-P acceptance letter and appraisals with the trust. BOE Prop 19 — continuing occupancy · SF 2026–27 increase

The B-specific wrinkles (SF triplex, already owned by the heir)

  • B is not part of the inheritance — it passes by existing ownership; no trust step, no reassessment on B itself at the transferor’s death. This is the one asymmetry that saves you $39,050/yr — but note: A’s death does not reassess B.
  • If B ever was the transferor’s principal residence, the exclusion could run on B’s occupied unit instead (and A would be fully reassessed). Confirm now which property carries the Homeowners’ Exemption.
  • SF Rent Board: tenant buyouts need care (just-cause rules); harassment/tenant-eviction exposure is real — keep the units tenanted, control-lawful increases, and document every notice.
  • If the heir sells A at inheritance and moves into B’s third unit, B’s character doesn’t change — the two controlled tenancies continue at lawful increases; the third unit becomes owner-occupied (fine; it was never tenanted).
Documents checklist to hand your attorney: trust + all amendments · deeds (both properties) · 2025–26 tax bills · insurance declarations · B leases · solar contract/warranty · transferor’s Homeowners’ Exemption claim (which property?) · beneficiary list + addresses (attorney drafts §16061.7 notice) · date-of-death appraisal orders. Bring the most recent itemised tax bills — the direct-charge lines are where reassessment disputes actually get won.
