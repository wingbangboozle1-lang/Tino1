2 · Decisive findings

  1. California charges $0 on inheritances — there is no state estate, inheritance or gift tax, and none at city/county level either (R&TC §13301). The real tax risk is (a) federal estate tax — only above $15,000,000/person, and (b) property-tax reassessment under Prop 19. State Controller · BOE Prop 19
  2. The federal exclusion is $15,000,000 per person in 2026 and “permanent” (Tax Cuts and Jobs Act sunset repealed by OBBBA, PL 119-21 §70106; indexed for inflation from 2027). Projected: $21,194,610 (2040) · $23,979,750 (2045) · $27,130,890 (2050) · $30,696,110 (2055). A & B combined (≈ $9,116,060 in 2045) fit under it by a wide margin; only other estate assets above ≈ $14,863,690 would trigger 40% tax. Rev. Proc. 2025-32 · OBBBA
  3. Step-up basis is the most valuable rule in this deal. IRC §1014 resets basis to date-of-death value, including revocable-trust property; a sale within ~1 year is effectively tax-free. Every year the property is held post-inheritance, more appreciation falls on top of the stepped-up basis and eventually faces ≈ 37.1% (federal 20% + 3.8% NIIT + California 13.3% incl. 1% Mental Health Services Tax) — or ≈ 41.1% on depreciation recapture for a rental. §1014 · IRS Topic 409 · FTB schedules · R&TC §17043
  4. Prop 19’s one-year clock is the single biggest controllable variable. Live-in within 12 months + BOE-266 (Homeowners’ Exemption) → A’s assessed value capped at FBYV + $1,521,770 (2045) = ≈ $4,437,980, tax ≈ $53,320/yr instead of $71,030. Miss the deadline → full reassessment, retroactively — no exceptions per BOE LTA 2026/026 Q7. File BOE-19-P within 3 years (late = prospective relief only). LTA 2026/026 · BOE Prop 19
  5. [Property B] is the income engine — and it gets reassessed no matter what. Rental units get no Prop 19 exclusion (only the unit the transferor actually occupied could qualify; multi-unit rules, LTA 2026/026 Q5/Q15). Bill jumps from ≈ $2,380 (2026) to ≈ $39,050 (2045). Net after tax + operating: still strongly positive (≈ $7,760/mo tax+ops vs $9,670/mo rent 2045). Keep both controlled units tenanted (rent control survives inheritance; 1.6% allowable increase 2026–27); the vacant unit can be occupied by the heir at zero rent — effectively reducing housing cost, not eliminating the tax. SF allowable increase · SF Treasurer
  6. Sell at inheritance ≈ 6 of 7 economic regimes (see Black swans). Terminal wealth 2060: baseline $19,840,500 (S1) vs $13,936,640 (S3) vs $11,260,090 (S2); stagflation is the lone flip (S3 $20,231,280). A diversified portfolio in 2026 is priced off a 4.9% 10-year Treasury and 3.7% CPI (Sept 2026) — not the 2%-inflation, 7%-return fantasy embedded in older forecasts. BLS CPI Sept 2026 · FOMC 2026-10-28
  7. Solar credit is policy-fragile — never bank it. The $10–20/mo net credit assumes grandfathered net-metering terms; one CPUC decision can zero it. Water $1,500/yr (A) and $4,380/yr (B), electricity ≈ $600/yr (B) — all user-supplied ranges, flagged. Insurance: Cupertino owner-occupied HO-3 est. ≈ $1,500/yr; SF landlord (3 units) ≈ $2,600/yr is an assumption, likely low — CDI comparison tool unreadable online; get real quotes. CA DOI premium tool · SFPUC/SJWC rate pages — see Costs
  8. Law is not static — monitor triggers. OBBBA made $15M “permanent” (a statute, not the Constitution); step-up basis has been targeted for repeal (2015 administration budget; 2021 American Families Plan $1M/$2.5M gains exclusion); CA ballot measures can tinker with Prop 13/19; SF Empty Homes Tax was struck down 2026-09-11 (Court of Appeal) but could be revived on further review. Track: Pending changes.

3 · Recommendation, as a plan

Baseline plan (2045 inheritance)

  1. Before death: nothing to file. Confirm A is the transferor’s principal residence on title + Homeowners’ Exemption claimed (only on one home); confirm both properties held 100% in the revocable trust (vesting deeds); confirm no other trust/LLC/partnership ownership (breaks step-up & Prop 19); insurance & titles current; gather date-of-death information needs (appraisal date) for step-up.
  2. At/near death: trustee provides §16061.7 notice within 60 days; file BOE-502-D change-in-ownership (death) with each county within 150 days (R&TC §480); file federal Form 706 only if taxable estate > exclusion — but file to elect portability if married transferor (5-year late-election window, Rev. Proc. 2022-32); date-of-death appraisals for A and B (required for step-up and Prop 19).
  3. Decision — sell or keep A: list A promptly if (i) proceeds grow at 7% nominal vs A’s 3.5% + you need diversification/liquidity, (ii) A’s new bill (≈ $53,320/yr even with Prop 19) exceeds budget, (iii) estate-liquidity needed for other assets. Default per model: sell; reinvest net ≈ $5,661,770 into a diversified portfolio (or pay down nondeductible debt).
  4. Decision — keep B: do not sell casually; it’s the income engine (≈ $63,840/yr net 2026 cash flow) and you can occupy the vacant unit rent-free. Retain; ensure both tenancies remain under control rules; consider furniture-free long-term leases; reassess insurance annually.
  5. Prop 19 housekeeping: if moving into A, file BOE-266 within 1 year of transfer; file BOE-19-P within 3 years (both counties/years). If planning to sell A anyway → skip Prop 19 (moot), skip Homeowners’ Exemption complexity, sell clean.
  6. If keeping A instead (values-led choice): understand the bill (≈ $53,320/yr at $4.44M assessed, growing 2%/yr, forever, minus Prop 8 declines) and re-run Scenarios; consider renting A long-term only as last resort — worst tax outcome ($71,030/yr) with ≈ 0.8% cash yield.
  7. Rebalance at death and at 5-year marks: estate liquidity vs property concentration; revisit insurance, roof/solar age, and whether any regime on that page has materialized (stagflation signal = sell-signal flips).
