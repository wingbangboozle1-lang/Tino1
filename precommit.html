Pre-commitment rules: what would make us choose each path

One rule per path, written and dated before the inheritance, so the decision is not reverse-engineered from one projection.

The point of this page

Agree in writing what would make the household choose each path, before the figures arrive. A rule written after the projection is reverse-engineered from that projection: the numbers are then chosen to justify the answer, instead of the answer being chosen because of the numbers. The fix is small and cheap - one sentence per path, written and dated now, with a number in it - and it is the only protection the decision has against its own author.

The table gives the model's break-even point for each path so the rule can be compared with something real. Writing the rule does not require knowing the answer: it requires deciding what would change your mind. Blank cells in the printed version are meant to stay blank until the household fills them in.

These are the model's own break-even points. The rule itself is the household's: it should be written down, dated and signed, and it should not be rewritten after the figures arrive.

How to use it

  1. Write one sentence per path. Name the number that would trigger it: a rent, an appreciation rate, a stock return, a date.
  2. Date it and keep it. The decision log records what was chosen and when. A rule with no date cannot be shown to have preceded the figures.
  3. Check the rule against the model once a year. The re-check calendar on the Law page says when each input is re-read. If the model's break-even moves past the rule's trigger, the rule is still the rule: either the facts changed the world, or the model changed. Both are worth knowing.
  4. Do not change a rule in the same week as a decision. If the rule must change, change it before the decision, not after.

The break-even points above are model outputs on the assumptions listed under Flagged for Review; they are conditional scenarios, not forecasts. The what-if controls let the household test the inputs directly.
