Glossary

Plain meanings of the official terms, each with the official page it comes from. Project terms are listed separately.

Official terms used in this analysis
TermPlain meaningWhy it matters hereOfficial basis
Base year valueThe assessed value a property gets when it changes ownership. Later years grow it by an inflation factor.It is the starting point for the Prop. 13 tax on Property A and Property B.R&TC 51
Annual cap of 2% on assessed valueThe inflation adjustment to a property's taxable value cannot exceed 2% of the prior year's value (R&TC 51, subparagraph D).Why Property B's taxable value grows slowly even while its market value rises.R&TC 51
Change in ownershipA transfer that can cause reassessment. A transfer by reason of death needs a statement filed within 150 days.Starts the 150-day filing clock for Property A.R&TC 480
Parent-child exclusion (Proposition 19)Keeps the old assessed value for a parent's home passed to a child only if the child makes it a principal residence, with a value limit.It decides whether Property A is reassessed when the Inheritor takes it.BOE Prop 19 · BOE LTA 2026/026
Principal residence (transferee)The home the child lives in as its main home, within a year of the transfer.Needed to keep the Prop. 19 exclusion for Property A.R&TC 63.2
Homeowners' exemptionA $7,000 reduction in taxable value for an owner-occupied home on the January 1 lien date. One claim (BOE-266).Needed before the Prop. 19 claim (BOE-19-P) can be filed.BOE homeowners' exemption · BOE Pub. 800-1
Basis (stepped-up at death)The starting value for tax on a later sale; for inherited property it is generally the fair market value on the date of death.Sets the gain if Property A is sold.IRS inherited-property FAQ
Irrevocable grantor trust (basis rule)Assets of an irrevocable grantor trust that are not in the deceased grantor's gross estate do not get the basis adjustment at death.Why the trust type is the first question (Q01).Rev. Rul. 2023-2
Federal basic exclusion amountThe amount each person can transfer without federal estate tax. $15,000,000 in 2026; $15,480,000 in 2027.Sets the room for cash and other assets after Property A.Form 706 instructions · Rev. Proc. 2026-35
Federal estate tax rateAbove the exclusion the tax is 40% of the excess (Table A, Form 706).Applies to the estate, not to the Inheritor.Form 706 instructions
Form 706The federal estate tax return. Due nine months after death; Form 4768 gives six more months.Filed if the estate is above the threshold or to keep portability.IRS estate FAQ
PortabilityPassing an unused exclusion to a surviving spouse; needs a timely Form 706.Checked for a predeceased spouse (Q02).IRS estate FAQ
Long-term capital gain rateNet capital gains are taxed at 0%, 15% or 20% depending on income.Tax on a later sale of stocks or Property A.IRS Topic 409
Unrecaptured section 1250 gainDepreciation taken on rental buildings is taxed at up to 25%.Applies to Property B and to Property A if rented.IRS Topic 409 · IRS Pub. 527
Depreciation (27.5 years)Residential rental buildings are depreciated straight-line over 27.5 years.Shelters rent; recaptured on sale.IRS Pub. 527
Home-sale exclusionUp to $250,000 ($500,000 joint) of gain on a main home, if used 2 of the last 5 years.Relevant if Property B is ever sold.IRS Topic 701
Net investment income tax3.8% on investment income, including rents and gains, above the threshold.Adds to the cost of renting or selling.IRS Topic 559
Like-kind exchangeDeferral of gain when real property held for investment is exchanged for like-kind property.One possible route for an investment property; not modelled.IRS like-kind exchanges
Supplemental tax billAn extra bill after a change in ownership for the period not yet taxed at the new value.Budgeted in every path (step 12 on the process page).SF Treasurer
Earthquake coverageHomeowners policies do not cover earthquake damage; a separate policy is needed.Not in the insurance estimate unless it is added (U02).CDI earthquake guide
Vacancy exclusion (60 days)Standard homeowners policies exclude losses to a house vacant for 60 or more days.Matters if a home is empty between death and sale.CDI residential guide
Tenant Protection Act (rent cap)State rent cap of 5% plus CPI, or 10% in total in 12 months, for covered units.Limits rent growth on Property B and on any rented Property A.CA Attorney General
San Francisco allowable rent increaseFor covered units the Rent Board allows 60% of the Bay Area CPI-U increase.Limits rent growth on Property B in the model (flag A10).SF rent increases
Net energy metering (NEM)The solar credit system. Under the 2014 decision, NEM 2.0 customers keep their tariff for 20 years from interconnection.Sets the solar credit at Property A (U05).CPUC net metering
Fixed monthly chargeThe utility's fixed Base Services Charge; $24.15 a month for standard Tier 3 customers.A fixed charge on each bill, separate from energy use (U05).CPUC fixed charge
3-month Treasury billA short-term U.S. government debt rate. Used as the cash parking return in the phased test.The reinvestment risk: cash earns the rate of the day.Treasury yields · FRED TB3MS (3-month Treasury bill)
2-year Treasury note (GS2)Market yield on 2-year U.S. Treasury notes, constant maturity.Cross-check for the parking return in 2007-2010.FRED GS2 (2-year Treasury)

Terms defined by this analysis (not from an official source)

Reinvestment risk
The chance that cash must be reinvested later at a lower rate. Shown next to the headline on the Phased entry page.
Black swan (in this analysis)
A sequence of real or invented economic conditions that departs from a constant growth and inflation rate, used as a stress test.
Phased entry
Parking the proceeds in short Treasuries for 24 or 36 months, then buying stocks in 12 equal monthly steps.
Mixed path
A combination of choices, such as living in Property A for a period and then renting or selling it. Not yet modelled on this site.
What-if case
One change to a baseline assumption, with the model rerun for it. Combined changes are not yet modelled.
