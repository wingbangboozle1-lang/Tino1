Property A figures assume the home IS included in the Transferor's gross estate, so the basis resets to market value at death and a prompt sale is nearly tax-free. If the trust sits outside the gross estate there is NO basis reset and a prompt sale is taxed on decades of gain - this single fact can reverse the entire plan (flag Q01). Have the estate attorney confirm it in writing before anything else.

5. Property B - keep exactly as it is

Property B is not part of the inheritance and nothing about Property A requires a change to it. Keeping it preserves a low assessed value ($116,540 against $1,800,000 market), worth about $19,890 a year of tax that is lost for good if it is sold. It already produces $85,800 a year from two rented units; the third unit is free for the Inheritor and no rent was ever supplied for it (baseline counts none - nothing is inferred). Selling Property B would also tax the whole historic gain because there is no basis reset on death of the Transferor.

8. Questions for the estate attorney

  • Confirm the trust type holding Property A and whether it will be included in the Transferor's gross estate (basis reset) - in writing.
  • Confirm the Transferor-Inheritor relationship meets the Proposition 19 parent-child definitions (BOE Pub. 800-1).
  • Was a Form 706 filed for a predeceased spouse, and is the portability election still available?
  • When does title actually pass, and could a marital trust, marital deduction or community-property title split the transfer?

9. Questions for the CPA / tax adviser

  • What is the Inheritor's real income-tax bracket (affects capital gains, NIIT and the sale-vs-rent comparison)?
  • Get a real insurance quote for each home - the premium is the weakest number on the site (owner estimate $1,500 a year each; official ZIP-level figure not published).
  • Are selling costs on a prompt sale of Property A deductible?
  • Should Form 706 be filed even below the threshold, to preserve portability?
