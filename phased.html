Phased entry: parking the proceeds before buying stocks

Does parking the sale proceeds in short Treasuries for 24 to 36 months soften the entry into a 1929-style or 2008-style crash, and what does it cost in a normal market?

Stock-market caveat: these are annual data (see flag H02). The phased path is a stress test of entry timing, not a forecast. The parking return is the 3-month bill (flag H01); the 2-year note is shown as a cross-check. Unverified: no official series covers the 1928-1933 bill and stock returns or the 2008 total returns (the linked FRED pages show their coverage), so those values are academic and flagged S02, not confirmed.

How the test works

  1. Starting cash: the model's after-tax net sale proceeds for Property A, $6,152,510 (rounded to $10).
  2. Lump sum: all of it in stocks on 1 January of the entry year.
  3. Phased: all of it in short Treasuries for 24 or 36 months, then 12 equal monthly purchases of stocks (flag H03).
  4. Every year's return is spread evenly over the months of that year; the 10-year horizon runs from the entry date.
