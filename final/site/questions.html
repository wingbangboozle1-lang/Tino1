Questions, unknowns, and discovery guide

Clear summary of known vs. unknown information, exact pre-decision questions, reading-dated decision register, and practical ways to find missing details without compromising privacy.

This page brings together what is already established, what cannot be verified from public records, the exact answers needed before making a final decision, and practical ways to uncover each missing piece without exposing private documents on the public repository.

Six blockers gate the decision. B1-B2: the trust instrument has never been seen and the basis treatment under Rev. Rul. 2023-2 is unconfirmed (Q01). B3: the estate size and any portability election are unknown against the $15,480,000 exclusion for 2027 (Q02). B4: the Inheritor's own income-tax bracket is unknown (Q05). B5: Proposition 19 parent-child eligibility is unconfirmed (Q06). B6: filing and claim deadlines need the date of death and the transfer timeline (Q15, Q16). Nothing on this page invents an answer: each row says what is known, what is missing, and the official basis.

Part 2 - decision register with reading dates

The short version. 14 questions are answered from official pages. 4 cannot be known until the time comes, and the model tests a range for each. 15 are open, and three of those matter far more than the rest. What the trust says about the home (D01) moves the result for selling by millions of dollars. Whether the Inheritor wants to live there (D02) is a personal choice with a known price. How sale proceeds would be invested (D03) is the one input that reverses the ranking in the cases tested: left in savings, selling falls behind renting. A decision is reasonable once those three are settled; the others refine the amounts.

Part 1 above (questions Q01 to Q14) and this part were written by two work sessions on the same day and are both kept. This part sorts the questions by how much they can move the result, gives the day each official page was last opened, and adds the questions that are already answered.
