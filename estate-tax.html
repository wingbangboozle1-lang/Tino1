Estate, inheritance & gift tax

What you can pass tax-free, what it costs above that, and how the thresholds drift 2026→2060. The short answer: California and its cities tax inheritances $0; federal tax only above $15,000,000 per person in 2026 — and OBBBA made that figure permanent.

2 · How much can you actually inherit tax-free — the direct answer

There is no tax penalty for inheriting an amount of money below the federal exclusion — and California adds nothing on top, at any level. In 2026 that threshold is $15,000,000 per person (US citizens & residents), doubled for a married couple with portability, indexed for inflation from 2027. The modest catch: “$15,000,000” means the entire estate — both properties + every bank/brokerage/retirement account + life insurance + lifetime taxable gifts. The two houses alone project to ≈ $9,116,060 in 2045, leaving ≈ $14,863,690 of headroom (2045) for everything else. If the “considerable amount of money” in the trust is under that headroom, the federal tax bill is $0 — not reduced, zero.

3 · Step-up in basis — the inheritance’s hidden 40% waiver

  • Basis resets to date-of-death FMV for property acquired from a decedent and for revocable-trust property (IRC §1014). CA community property (if any) gets a double step-up on the decedent’s half and the surviving spouse’s half (IRC §1014(b)(6); R&TC conforms).
  • Selling A or B within ~12 months of death: gain ≈ $0, any rate ≈ 0. Waiting: appreciation after death is taxed at up to ≈ 37.1% combined; for a rental, depreciation taken (even if none claimed) creates unrecaptured §1250 gain at up to 25% federal + CA ordinary rates + 3.8% NIIT → ≈ 41.1% blended.
  • Do not “hold for basis”: there is no scenario where waiting to sell improves the basis — the step-up is already locked in at death.
  • If part of the plan is gifting A or B before death: gifts carry carryover basis (IRC §1015) — gifting appreciated property destroys the step-up forever. Keep appreciated property in the trust until death.

4 · “Permanent” is a statute, not a law of nature — what could change

  • OBBBA §70106 (July 2025) made $15,000,000 permanent — previously it was scheduled to halve after 2025 (TCJA sunset). A future Congress can still cut it (as it did temporarily 2013) or add a surtax (e.g., proposals of 45%/25% above $10M/$20M discussed 2023–25). Probability: low near-term; not zero. See Pending changes.
  • Step-up repeal has been proposed (2015 Administration budget; 2021 American Families Plan — tax gains > $1,000,000/$2,500,000 couples at death at capital-gain rates + §1014 repeal going forward). It failed, but the coalition for it persists; a change would matter most for heirs planning to hold A long-term.
  • California: no estate/inheritance/gift tax today; ballot-measure risk exists historically (e.g., 2012 Prop 30/55 high-income tax extension — Prop 3 passing unofficially Nov 3, 2026 per SOS unofficial returns; certification due Dec 11, 2026). CA wealth-tax proposals have repeatedly failed to qualify or pass; none enacted.
  • GST exemption ≈ same $15,000,000; relevant only if property skips to grandchildren.

5 · What to do with the threshold headroom (planning levers, not advice)

  1. If total estate < exclusion: nothing to file; optional Form 706 for portability if the transferor is married. Keep the date-of-death appraisals — they anchor basis, Prop 19 and any future sale.
  2. If within ~$1M of exclusion: annual gifting of $19,000/recipient from the estate; spousal portability; consider ILI for any estate-tax funding needs (life insurance on the survivor can move the estate below threshold).
  3. If above exclusion: the correct tool is an attorney-drafted plan (credit-shelter trust, GRAT, dynasty trust, charitable lead trust), not a spreadsheet. 40% on the excess is the only “inheritance penalty” that exists.
  4. Never let the existence of “considerable money” be measured against the wrong number — the exclusion is per person, combined across lifetime gifts + estate, and unchanged by the properties’ Prop 13 basis.
