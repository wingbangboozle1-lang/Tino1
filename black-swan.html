Black swans & regime risk, 2026–2070

Growth and inflation have never compounded smoothly for 50 years in any economy on record — not in the US, not in Japan, not anywhere. This page replaces the fiction of a straight-line projection with (a) what actually happened since 1929, (b) seven named regimes applied to the 2045 inheritance through 2060, and (c) a playbook for the outcome nobody forecast.

Why this page exists. Every “smooth” 3% appreciation / 2.5% CPI projection hides the same lie: the last 50 years contained three >40% drawdowns, two inflation spikes >9%, a 14-year Japanese bear market, a pandemic recession two months long, and 2026’s energy-led re-acceleration to 3.7% CPI. A 50-year plan that only models the median case is a plan for the median world — which has never existed. The correct response is not a fake 95% confidence interval; it is robustness across regimes plus explicit tripwires.

Parameters are assumptions, not forecasts (see Methodology ± sensitivity). The point is not “which number is right” — it is that the S1-vs-S3 ranking flips exactly once: when equities return ≈ CPI for 15 years (stagflation). In that world, hard assets (kept A, plus B) win. All other regimes — deflation, stagnation, crash, normal, boom — favor selling A at the step-up and diversifying. A portfolio of broad equities has ~1 negative-15-year window since 1900 (Japan 1990–2020); US equity has never delivered a negative nominal 15-year total return.

4 · The playbook — surviving the unsurvivable

  1. Sell the concentrated position at the moment of maximum tax efficiency (death). The step-up is the only guaranteed, legislated “free” revaluation you will ever get. Concentration is optional afterward; the basis reset is not.
  2. Ladder the reinvestment. Proceeds split: 2 years spending / T-bills & money-market; core in diversified index equity (60–70%) + TIPS (10–20%, stagflation hedge) + munis (if CA resident) ; alternatives ≤ 10%. Not advice — structure to be reviewed with a fiduciary.
  3. Keep B. A paid-off, rent-controlled, high-yield (≈ 6–7% cash-on-value today) asset is the anti-fragile leg; refinancing optional, never required.
  4. Pre-commit to tripwires (write them into the trust or a family memo): (a) CPI > 5% y/y for 2 consecutive quarters → revisit “sell vs hold” verdict (stagflation flip risk); (b) 10-yr Treasury > 6% → do not sell A into a frozen market, wait 6 months; (c) Case-Shiller SF y/y < −10% → pause any non-urgent sale, collect rent instead; (d) any Prop 13/19 amendment on the ballot → re-run this whole analysis within 30 days; (e) insurance non-renewal arrives → bind FAIR Plan + surplus lines within 30 days, never let coverage lapse.
  5. Liquidity before legacy. After the sale, keep ≥ 18 months of household costs + both properties’ taxes/insurance in cash/T-bills — inheritance years fail families at tax deadlines (Form 706 due 9 months after death if taxable), not at strategy.
  6. Write the obituary test. If the plan only works when nothing goes wrong, it is not a plan. Every number above survives deflation, Japan-stagnation and GFC; one survives stagflation only by holding A. Know which world you think you live in — and let the sell/hold tripwire, not ego, decide.
