Inheritance process (step by step)

From the Transferor's death to a settled title, with every statutory deadline and form.

WhenWhat to doWhy / consequenceOfficial source
Before deathTransferor keeps both properties titled in the revocable trust; keeps the Homeowners' Exemption on the home actually occupied; keeps records of basis, improvements and rental depreciation; names a successor trustee.Preserves the §1014 step-up and the Prop 19 'family home' status; avoids probate.26 U.S.C. §1014 · BOE HOX
Day 0 — deathTrust becomes irrevocable; change in ownership for property-tax purposes occurs on this date (R&TC §61(h)); values on this date set the income-tax basis and the Prop 19 full cash value.Order date-of-death appraisals for both properties.BOE Prop 19
Within 60 daysSuccessor trustee serves the Probate Code §16061.7 notice on all beneficiaries and heirs (starts the 120-day contest period). Obtain death certificates; get an EIN for the now-irrevocable trust; open a trust account.Statutory duty of the trustee.Prob. Code §16061.7
Within 150 daysFile BOE-502-D (Change in Ownership Statement — Death of Real Property Owner) with the Santa Clara County Assessor (A) and the San Francisco Assessor-Recorder (B); record affidavits of death of trustee / trustee's deeds as needed. Transfers by reason of death are exempt from documentary transfer tax (R&TC §11930).Late filing penalty: greater of $100 or 10% of the new tax if not filed within 90 days of the assessor's request.R&TC §480 · R&TC §11930
Within 1 yearIf the Heir will keep Property A as a home: move in and file BOE-266 (Homeowners' Exemption) with the Santa Clara County Assessor. There is no extension to this deadline.Required for any Prop 19 exclusion; also claims the $7,000 exemption.BOE LTA 2026/026 Q7 · SCC Assessor
Within 9 months (+6 month extension)File federal Form 706 only if the gross estate plus adjusted taxable gifts exceeds the year-of-death exclusion ($15 million in 2026, indexed) — or to elect portability for a surviving spouse. If filed, Form 8971 basis statements go to the heirs.Most likely not required for the two properties alone; required if other assets are large.IRS estate FAQs · IRS deceased person
By April 15 of the following yearTrustee files the Transferor's final Form 1040 / 540 and, if the trust received rent or sale proceeds, Form 1041 / 541 for the trust.Rents from Property B after death belong to the trust until distribution.IRS deceased person
Within 3 yearsFile BOE-19-P (parent–child exclusion claim) for Property A with the Santa Clara County Assessor. Late claims receive prospective relief only. San Francisco's Assessor follows the same forms for Property B's owner-occupied unit if applicable.Secures the exclusion back to the date of death.BOE Prop 19 · SF Assessor fact sheet
When supplemental bills arriveBoth counties issue supplemental assessments for the reassessed value from the date of death; pay by the Dec 10 / Apr 10 delinquency dates (10% penalty).Budget for the jump shown on the Projections page.SF TTX · SCC rate book
DistributionTrustee distributes the properties (or sale proceeds) per the trust; record deeds from trustee to Heir (exempt from transfer tax as a distribution under a trust by reason of death). Heir's holding period is automatically long-term.Then execute the chosen scenario.R&TC §11930
If sellingList soon after the appraisal so the sale price ≈ stepped-up basis (minimal gain). Selling costs: ~5% commissions/closing (assumption) + transfer tax (SF 0.75%; Santa Clara County 0.11%). For a rental sold later: depreciation recapture at up to 25% federal + California ordinary rates.Property B tenants keep their tenancies through a sale (rent ordinance).SF transfer tax · IRS 409
If keeping B as a rentalRegister with the SF Rent Board as required, follow the annual allowable increase (1.6% for Mar 2026–Feb 2027), keep all units occupied (Empty Homes Tax risk if revived), report rents on Schedule E with depreciation.Owner move-in into an occupied unit is a just-cause eviction with strict conditions — the vacant unit avoids this.SF rental laws · EHT
Not covered here: trust-contest procedures, creditor claims, Medi-Cal estate recovery, and non-California assets. A California estate attorney should run the administration; the deadlines above are the ones that cost money when missed.
