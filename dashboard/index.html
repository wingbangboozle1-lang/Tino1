Dashboard

A self-contained research and decision dashboard for inheriting two California properties through a revocable trust. All figures rounded to the nearest $10.

Headline findings
  1. No estate tax is expected on the two properties alone. They are worth about $4.9 million today and roughly $8,090,860–$11,605,270 in 2040–2050 (base appreciation), versus a federal exclusion of $15 million in 2026 that is indexed for inflation (about $21,194,610 per person by 2040 at 2.5%/yr). California has no estate or inheritance tax. Estate tax only becomes relevant if other assets push the estate above the exclusion — see the exposure table.
  2. Inheriting (not gifting) gives a full step-up in basis (26 U.S.C. §1014): decades of appreciation become tax-free if the heir sells soon after death. This is the single most valuable tax feature of the plan.
  3. Prop 19 helps far less than most people expect. Only the transferor's principal residence qualifies, the heir must move in within one year, and the excluded amount is capped at the factored base value plus about $1.04–1.6 million. For Property A (base case, 2045) the assessed value still jumps from about $786,300 to $4,979,030 and the tax bill from $10,500 to $59,240 per year. Property B's rental units get no exclusion at all and are reassessed to full market value ($38,750/yr in 2045).
  4. Keeping a reassessed $6–7 million house is expensive: about $75,780 per year of carrying cost in 2045 for Property A alone. Selling it at the stepped-up basis (little or no capital-gains tax) and investing the proceeds produces the highest after-tax wealth in 25 of 27 sensitivity cases; keeping both properties wins only when appreciation is high (≥6%/yr) and investment returns are low (≤5%).
  5. Recommended plan (base case): Live in B's vacant unit, sell A and invest — after-tax wealth of $14,218,760 in 2055 versus $11,785,580 for living in A and keeping B. The lifestyle difference (a 2-bedroom flat vs a large Cupertino house) is real and is priced in the scenario page; if the heir wants to live in Property A, the next-best plan is to live in A and sell B (S2).
  6. The recommendation survives black swan economics. Re-running the decision under six economic paths built from real history (1970s stagflation, the 2006–12 Bay Area crash of −23% to −35%, a Japan-style deflation tail, and the 2020–23 whiplash) over 50 variable years, selling at inheritance wins 12 of 18 stress cells and "live in it" wins only the housing-boom/replay cells — see the Black swan section.
