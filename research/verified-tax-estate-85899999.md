## 2. California Proposition 19 Intergenerational Transfer Rules (`RTC § 63.2`)

For transfers between parents and children (or eligible grandparents and grandchildren) occurring on or after **February 16, 2021**, **California Constitution Article XIII A, § 2.1(c)–(e)**, **RTC § 63.2**, and **BOE Property Tax Rule 462.520** establish strict limits on avoiding property tax reassessment upon trust inheritance:

1. **Principal Residence Requirement (Both Generations):**
   - The property **must** have been the **principal residence of the `[Grantor/Settlor]`** prior to death/transfer, **AND**
   - At least one eligible **`[Beneficiary]`** must move into the property as their **principal residence within one (1) year** of the date of death/transfer and file for the **Homeowners' Exemption (`Form BOE-266`, `$7,000` assessed value exemption under `RTC § 218`)** or Disabled Veterans' Exemption within 1 year, plus file **`Form BOE-19-P`** (*Claim for Reassessment Exclusion for Transfer Between Parent and Child*).
   - **Sources:** [CA BOE Pub 801 — Prop 19 Fact Sheet](https://www.boe.ca.gov/pdf/pub801.pdf); [CA BOE LTA 2025/015](https://boe.ca.gov/proptaxes/pdf/lta25015.pdf); [CA BOE Sample Form BOE-19-P](https://www.boe.ca.gov/proptaxes/pdf/sample-boe19p.pdf).
2. **Value Cap Formula (Partial Reassessment Above Cap):**
   - Excluded Value Limit = `Factored Base Year Value (FBYV) + Inflation-Adjusted $1,000,000 Exclusion Cap`.
   - For transfers occurring **February 16, 2025 through February 15, 2027**, the official BOE exclusion cap is **`$1,044,590`** (rounded to nearest `$10`; exact statutory BOE figure `$1,044,586` per LTA 2025/009, adjusted biennially by the FHFA House Price Index for California).
   - If `Fair Market Value (FMV)` at death exceeds `FBYV + Exclusion Cap`, the **excess FMV is added directly to FBYV** to establish the new taxable value:
     `New Taxable Value = FBYV + max($0, FMV - (FBYV + Exclusion Cap))` — which algebraically simplifies whenever `FMV > FBYV + Exclusion Cap` to **`FMV - Exclusion Cap`**!
   - **Critical Mathematical Insight for `[Property A — Cupertino 95014]`:** Whenever `FMV` exceeds `FBYV + Exclusion Cap` (which is already true in 2026 at `$3,100,000` FMV vs. `$550,540` FBYV + `$1,044,590` cap = `$1,595,130` threshold, and remains true through 2040–2050), `FBYV` cancels out! Moving into `[Property A]` under Prop 19 reduces the reassessed taxable value by exactly the **Exclusion Cap** (projected at ~`$1,480,000`–`$1,890,000` by 2040–2050 assuming ~2.5%–3.0%/yr FHFA HPI biennial growth), saving ~`$17,220`–`$21,990/yr` in property taxes compared to renting out `[Property A]`, **but still increasing `[Property A]` property taxes dramatically** above the pre-inheritance Prop 13 bill!
   - **Sources:** [CA BOE Pub 801](https://www.boe.ca.gov/pdf/pub801.pdf); [CA BOE LTA 2025/015](https://boe.ca.gov/proptaxes/pdf/lta25015.pdf).
3. **Loss of Exclusion Upon Moving Out or Renting (`Rule 462.520`):**
   - If the `[Beneficiary]` rents out `[Property A]` upon inheritance instead of occupying it as principal residence within 1 year, **0% of Prop 19 applies** — `[Property A]` is reassessed to **100% of Fair Market Value** as of the date of death (generating supplemental and ongoing secured tax bills under `SB 813`).
   - If the `[Beneficiary]` initially moves in to claim Prop 19 and **later moves out** (without another eligible sibling beneficiary moving in within 1 year), the exclusion is **removed** on the next January 1 lien date, and taxable value resets to the date-of-death FMV compounded by up to 2%/yr CCPI for the elapsed years.
   - **Sources:** [CA BOE LTA 2025/015](https://boe.ca.gov/proptaxes/pdf/lta25015.pdf); [CA BOE LTA 2026/026](https://www.boe.ca.gov/proptaxes/pdf/lta26026.pdf).
4. **Single Primary Residence Conflict & Multi-Unit Property Rule:**
   - Under `RTC § 218` and `Rule 462.520`, an individual (or married couple living together) can claim **only one principal residence / Homeowners' Exemption** at a time. Therefore, if `[Beneficiary]` lives in the 3rd unit of `[Property B — San Francisco 94122]` as their primary residence, `[Beneficiary]` **cannot** claim Prop 19 on `[Property A — Cupertino 95014]`.
   - Furthermore, under `LTA 2026/026` and `Rule 462.520`, each unit of a multi-unit building (such as the 3-unit `[Property B — San Francisco 94122]`) is treated as a **separate residence**; non-ADU rental units never qualify for the Prop 19 parent-child exclusion on a change in ownership.
   - **Source:** [CA BOE LTA 2026/026](https://www.boe.ca.gov/proptaxes/pdf/lta26026.pdf).
