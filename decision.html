Why selling wins on the numbers

  • The tax base is lost either way. Property A is taxed on $802,030 just before inheritance. Afterwards it is taxed on $6,531,230 if rented, and still on $5,031,080 if the Inheritor moves in, because the Proposition 19 allowance ($1,500,150 by then) is small next to a $6.53 million home.
  • The basis reset makes the sale almost tax-free once. That is worth about $2,095,020 of income tax compared with a sale without the reset. It is used in full by selling; it is used slowly, through depreciation, by renting.
  • The rent is small next to the value. $127,740 of rent on a $6.53 million home is a gross yield of 2.0%, and the reassessed property tax alone takes 61% of it.
  • Property B already supplies housing and a protected tax base. The Inheritor does not need Property A to live in, and Property B cannot be replaced at its tax cost.

When the answer changes

  • If the proceeds would be kept in savings or Treasury bills, renting comes out ahead.
  • If the home appreciates 7.0% a year or more for decades (it did 6.7% a year from 1976 to 2025) while stocks earn 7%, renting catches up.
  • If the trust does not give a basis reset, every choice is worse and the gap between selling and renting narrows.
  • If the Inheritor wants to live in Property A for reasons that are not financial, the cost is shown above and the Proposition 19 claim must be filed in time (see Process).
