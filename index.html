Best plan and why - conditional on the assumptions in the flags

  1. Keep Property B and keep living in its third unit. Its assessed value is $116,540 against a market value of $1,800,000, which saves about $19,890 of property tax every year, and that saving is lost for good if it is sold. It also has no basis reset, so selling it would tax the whole gain. It already produces $85,800 a year.
  2. Sell Property A within a few months of inheriting it and invest the proceeds in a diversified stock fund. With the basis reset to the date-of-death value the sale has no taxable gain (the model shows a small loss of $333,750 from selling costs). About $6,152,510 is released. In the baseline it grows to $33,502,220 by 2076 after all taxes ($9,747,210 in 2026 dollars), against $19,187,480 if rented and $11,844,960 if lived in.
  3. Expect the first ten years to be the risky ones. Replaying the real 1976-2025 record from each of its 50 starting years, selling is ahead of renting in 43 of 50 cases ten years after the inheritance and in all 50 after twenty. In the worst ten-year case selling is behind by $2,069,690 (2026 dollars). A long stagnation of the kind Japan had after 1990 is the worst tested path for selling into stocks: renting ends ahead in it, and renting also edges ahead in the 1929-1941 Depression path - the only two named paths where selling into stocks loses to renting (see the Black swan section). Investing the proceeds in stages and keeping a few years of spending in Treasury bills narrows that risk.
  4. Do not sell and park the money in savings. If the proceeds would sit in savings or Treasury bills at about 3.5%, keeping the house as a rental comes out ahead ($5,118,360 against $3,554,580 in 2026 dollars).
  5. Living in Property A is a lifestyle choice with a price. The assessed value jumps from $802,030 to $5,031,080 even with the Proposition 19 relief, so the tax bill rises from about $10,820 to about $59,950 a year. Compared with selling, living there costs about $6,301,010 of 2076 wealth in 2026 dollars.
  6. Before anything else, have the trust read by an estate attorney. The whole result depends on the home being included in the Transferor's gross estate so that the basis resets. If it is not, selling triggers tax on decades of gain (the model shows $3,319,060 less wealth in 2026 dollars). See the Flagged for Review page.

Wealth counts only what differs between the choices: Property A itself, every cash flow it causes, and rent from the Inheritor's vacated unit in Property B when the Inheritor moves to Property A. How to read the numbers. Three kinds of figures appear on this site. Official facts are quoted from the government page linked on the row. Owner-supplied figures (market values, rents, utilities, tax bills, insurance estimates) are the owner's own numbers: they are rounded to the nearest $10 for privacy, cannot be confirmed on an official page (flag P03), and the link on their row shows only the rule or index the calculation follows, not verification of the figure. Model outputs are projections from the assumptions on the Flagged for Review page - conditional scenarios, not forecasts. Every headline fact was re-read on its official page in three independent passes, on 2026-11-11 and twice on 2026-11-12: see the Verification register.
