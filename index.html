Two-home inheritance decision dashboard

Property A (Santa Clara County, [ZIP A]) will pass to the Inheritor through a trust some time around 2040 to 2050. Property B (San Francisco, [ZIP B]) is already owned by the Inheritor. This site works out, with an official source on every table row, whether to live in Property A, rent it out, or sell it and reinvest.

Privacy: no names, street addresses, parcel numbers or account numbers appear anywhere on this site. People are "the Transferor" and "the Inheritor". Every owner-supplied and modelled dollar figure is rounded to the nearest $10, which is the disclosure-minimisation control the brief asks for. Rounding is not anonymisation, and this repository is public: options G01 and G02 on the Suggestions page set out the narrower figure-free boundary if you would rather use it.

The answer on one screen

Headline (baseline, inheritance in 2045, basis reset)

On the 2076 measure the baseline ranks sell and invest first. After-tax wealth in 2026 dollars, 10 years after inheritance: sell and invest $4,983,420, rent 5 years then sell $4,551,830, rent for good $4,171,650, live for good $3,473,790. Selling beats renting in 43 of 50 real starting years after 10 years.

Reinvestment risk, beside the headline

Worst of the 50 real starting years for selling: $3,705,880 at 10 years (start year 1980), against $5,774,590 for renting from the same year. Median of the 50 starts: $9,897,780. Parking the proceeds for 24 months first costs up to -$2,771,730 in the worst start and -$650,360 in the median, at 10 years (2026 dollars).

What would change the answer

  1. No basis reset: if the trust keeps Property A outside the estate, selling triggers income tax on decades of gain (flag Q01, flag Q02).
  2. Moving in and claiming the parent-child relief in time keeps the property tax low; renting or leaving it empty reassesses it (BOE Prop 19).
  3. Stock returns and inflation far from the baseline: see What-if controls (ranges and sources shown).
  4. Law changes before the inheritance (flag A13, Rev. Proc. 2026-35): nothing decades ahead can be verified today.
