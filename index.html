Executive Summary — Should we live in, rent, or sell the Cupertino house?

Two California properties feed one decision: keep the San Francisco Outer Sunset three-unit as our base, and choose what to do when the Cupertino house (95014, ~$3.1M in Nov 2026) passes by revocable living trust around 2040–2050. This hub collects, verifies, and projects every line item — property tax (Prop 13 / Prop 19), insurance, utilities, rent, capital gains, and reinvestment — so you can see the profit-maximizing path with clear numbers.

Bottom line recommendation

If you inherit around 2045 and can make Cupertino your primary residence within 12 months: live in it; rent the SF house fully. If you cannot: sell Cupertino promptly after the stepped-up basis and reinvest — holding it as a non-primary second home triggers a near-full property-tax reassessment that erases the rental advantage.

Why: Prop 19 (effective Feb 16 2021) keeps a low Prop 13 base only for a parent's principal residence that becomes the child's principal residence within 1 year, and only up to the factored base-year value + $1,044,586 (Feb 16 2025–Feb 15 2027, adjusted biennially). Above that, the home is partially reassessed; if you don't move in, it is fully reassessed to market (county assessor FY2025-26 rate ~1.16% Cupertino / 1.18% SF). See Tax Law and Sources.

At a glance — what drives the decision

  • No CA estate/inheritance tax; federal estate tax only above $15M per person / $30M couple in 2026 (made permanent by P.L. 119-21). [IRS]
  • Step-up in basis (IRC §1014): heirs sell with basis = market at death; lifetime gains wiped. [IRS Pub 551]
  • Cupertino effective rate ~1.16% of assessed; median home tax ~$15,340/yr on ~$1.32M assessed (SCC Assessor 2025). SF 1.1827% FY25-26. [SCC / SF Treasurer]
  • Home price growth 3.6% statewide (C.A.R. 2026 forecast); Bay Area 2–6% [C.A.R.]
  • Insurance up 5–7% / yr in CA — verify quotes annually (CDI/NAIC). [CDI]

When to LIVE IN it

Live-in wins if …

  • You genuinely want to live in Cupertino as primary residence ≥2 yrs (also unlocks §121 $250k/$500k exclusion on later sale).
  • Combined SF rent ($7,150 → ~$12k/mo by 2045 at 3%) covers both properties' carrying costs.
  • You value stability over liquidity.
When to RENT it

Renting is weakest

  • Full tax reset almost wipes out rent profit; CA taxes rent as ordinary income (up to 13.3%).
  • Bay Area rent control / vacancy risk.
  • Only makes sense if you cannot sell (e.g., trust restrictions) and rents surge >5%/yr.
When to SELL & REINVEST

Sell wins if …

  • You won't live in Cupertino (no Prop 19 shield).
  • You prefer diversified, liquid wealth and lower annual costs.
  • You can use stepped-up basis to sell tax-free and let compounding work (6–7% vs ~3.5% home appreciation minus ~1.3% tax drag).
