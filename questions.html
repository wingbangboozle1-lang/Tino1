A clear list of questions that must be answered before any decision can be reasonably made — what we have, what we do not have, the answer if known, and suggestions for how to discover missing information.

A reasonable decision needs answers, not just projections. This page puts every question in one place: what information we already have, what we do not have, the question that must be answered, the answer if known, and a concrete suggestion for how to discover the missing information. Nothing here asks you to type values into the site — each suggestion says who to ask and what paper to gather.

Decision-readiness checklist — you can reasonably decide once you have

  • Legal: ☐ Trust inclusion letter (Q01) — without it, basis reset is assumption. ☐ Property-tax deadlines understood (60-day notice, 150-day BOE statement, 1-year HOX). See Process and Law.
  • Tax: ☐ Estate total vs $15,480,000 exclusion room (Q02) — determines if cash triggers 40% above line. ☐ Your actual income-tax bracket (Q05) — determines after-tax rent vs sell gap. ☐ Prop 19 eligibility (Q06) confirmed.
  • Costs: ☐ Written insurance quotes for 95014 and 94122 (U01) + earthquake decision (U02). ☐ Actual tax bills confirming tax rate area (U03). ☐ Solar NEM date/tariff (U05). ☐ Rent opinion for A (U08) with bedroom count.
  • Values: ☐ Appraisal or comparable sales for $3,100,000 / $1,800,000 estimates. ☐ Basis in Property B (Q03) if selling B is ever considered.
  • Model sanity: ☐ Read Decision sensitivities (break-even home growth and stock return) and Black swan (50 replays, Japan and Depression paths where rent wins). If you cannot accept the worst 10-year case behind by $2,069,690, choose a staged investment and 2-year bill buffer.
  • Privacy: ☐ Decide repository visibility — rounding to $10 does not anonymize a household; Git history stays public until you make the repo private or rewrite history flag P02.

Check each box by gathering the document in “How to discover.” When every legal box and the cost boxes are checked, the three choices on the Decision page are comparable with verified inputs rather than estimates. Until then, the site marks the missing items as flags and shows the result conditional on the assumptions.
