Cash, estate and inheritance tax

The threshold at each level of government, the room before federal tax, and a projection to 2050 - including the part of the inheritance that is not a house.

The inheritance is two things. Property A, and a cash amount. The cash matters for the estate tax calculation even though it is not real estate, because the federal tax is computed on the gross estate as a whole. The tables below show the room before tax at three transfer dates and five estate sizes.

No inheritance tax at state or local level

California imposes no estate tax and no inheritance tax for deaths on or after 1 January 2005, and no California generation-skipping tax on transfers after 31 December 2004. The only transfer tax at death is federal, and it is paid by the estate, not by the heir. The table on the tax and law page links the State Controller's own statement.

What the projection shows

The room is large but not unlimited

At a 2045 transfer the projected exclusion is $24,143,520 and the house accounts for $6,531,230 of it, leaving $17,612,280 of headroom. Cash above that headroom is taxed at 40%. The projection is not a prediction of the law: it assumes the exclusion keeps being indexed. The 2026 and 2027 amounts are official; everything after them is a projection.

The house consumes headroom whichever path is chosen

The house is in the estate whether it is later sold, rented or lived in. Selling it after the transfer does not remove it from the gross estate: the estate is measured at the date of death. That is why the estate calculation does not depend on the decision this site is about - but it does decide whether the estate has to sell something to pay the tax.

Liquidity is the real risk

An estate tax bill is due in cash, generally nine months after death, and the estate may hold mostly real estate. The options are to sell the house, borrow against it, or use life insurance held outside the estate. This is a question for the trustee and the estate attorney, and it is question Q02 in the register.

Portability is the other lever

If the transferor had a spouse who died earlier, the unused exclusion of that spouse can be ported only if a timely estate-tax return was filed for the first death. If it was not, the estate may have half the room it thought it had. The executor controls this election and it cannot be made after the fact.

Gifts before death are part of the estate. Taxable gifts made during life are added back when the estate tax is computed. A plan that gives assets away shortly before death does not reduce the estate; it only moves assets.
