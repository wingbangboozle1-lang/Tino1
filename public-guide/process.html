Practical roadmap / 05

From trust to decision

Every step from reading the trust to recording the deed, in order, with the statutory clock that governs each one.

This is a sequence, not legal advice. The order matters because three of the steps have statutory clocks that run from the same event, and one of them - the homeowners' exemption - is also an eligibility condition for the other. Missing it does not just cost an exemption; it can cost the whole intergenerational relief.

The claim must be filed within three years of the transfer, or before the property is transferred to a third party, whichever is earlier.BOE-LTA-2026-026 · SCC-P19

The three clocks that matter

60 days - tell the beneficiaries

The trustee must serve the statutory notification within 60 days of the event that makes the trust irrevocable, or of a change of trustee. This is the first clock and it is about information, not tax.

1 year - the exemption and the occupancy

Two conditions run on the same one-year clock: the transferee must file for the homeowners' exemption, and the home must become the transferee's principal residence. Both are conditions of the Proposition 19 exclusion, not optional extras.

3 years - the transfer claim

The BOE-19-P claim must be filed within three years of the transfer, or before a transfer to a third party if that comes first. Selling the home before filing can end the relief.

What this means for the plan

The recommendation is to sell within the year. The process is consistent with that: the claim and the exemption are filed, the assessor rules, and the sale follows. What the plan must not do is sell first and file later, because the claim is a condition of the relief and not a formality.
