Sources and method

Every page read, every assumption used, what was verified directly and what was not - so that any number on this site can be traced or rejected.

How this edition was built. A single Python generator reads one model output file and writes every page. The generator has no access to any document, address or bill: the only household inputs it can see are the model's rounded parameters and results. That is a structural privacy control, not a promise.

What was verified directly, and what was not

Verified on 2026-11-13, in this session

  • The federal estate-tax filing-threshold table, including $15,000,000 for 2026.
  • The 2027 inflation adjustments, including a $15,480,000 exclusion and a $20,000 annual gift exclusion, read from the revenue procedure itself rather than from a summary.
  • That California has no estate or inheritance tax for deaths on or after 1 January 2005.
  • The Proposition 19 comparison and the intergenerational transfer FAQ, including the three-year claim, the one-year occupancy and the continual-use rule.
  • The value cap of $1,044,586 for transfers through 15 February 2027.
  • The parent-child worked example published by the assessor, which the model reproduces.
  • The homeowners' exemption and its filing deadline.
  • The statutory 2% cap and the California consumer price index as the factor.
  • The county rate book: 1% general levy plus voter-approved charges, and the instalment deadlines.
  • The city secured rate for fiscal 2026-27: 1.18162182%.
  • The 1.6% allowable rent increase for covered units from 1 March 2026.
  • Rent control coverage for units built on or before 13 June 1979.
  • The Statewide rent cap under the civil code.
  • HUD's FY2026 small-area fair-market rents for both ZIP codes, at every bedroom size.
  • The net-metering tariff structure, including the 20-year NEM 2.0 transition period and the fixed charge.
  • The insurance regulator's residential guide, including the exclusions.
  • The federal budget office's inflation path, as context for the baseline.
  • The Federal Reserve History account of the Great Inflation.
  • That the city's empty-homes tax is not being enforced after the appellate decision.
  • The trust notification deadline and the basis rule for inherited property.

Not verified, and said so

  • Any insurance premium. No quote was obtained; all premiums are placeholders.
  • The current assessed values and the bill history. Supplied by the owner, withheld from this edition by design, and not independently confirmed.
  • The market value of either property. An estimate, not an appraisal.
  • The rent for Property A. A published benchmark, not a valuation.
  • The solar interconnection terms. The agreement was not read.
  • Estate and legal fees. Not modelled.
  • The future Proposition 19 allowance. Not published yet.
  • Any future tax law. Only the law in force is stated.

Where the earlier editions are

Earlier editions are retained in the repository and are not republished here. The original no-figures guide remains live and is linked from the front door; this edition is additive.

The professional help this site is not. Nothing here is legal, tax, investment or insurance advice. The site prepares the questions and the arithmetic for a licensed estate attorney, a tax adviser and an insurance broker, and it is written so that each of them can check a number rather than take it on trust.

De-identification method. People, the trust, the parcels and the addresses appear only as placeholders and are never published. Household amounts are household or model outputs rounded to the nearest $10 and are labelled as such; statutory amounts are quoted exactly as the official page publishes them and are labelled official. No year-by-year bill history is published. Nothing on this site is legal, tax, investment or insurance advice.
