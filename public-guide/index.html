Recommendation, in one paragraph

Keep Property B, stay in its third unit, and sell Property A, then invest the proceeds in a diversified stock fund. The model puts selling $4.16M ahead of renting and $7.49M ahead of living in it by 2076, in 2026 dollars. Selling wins 50 of 50 replays of the real 1976-2025 record after twenty years and 43 of 50 after ten - so the plan is strongest on a horizon of twenty years or more. The recommendation is conditional: it fails if the home is outside the gross estate (no basis reset), if the estate is large enough for federal tax that must be paid from the house, if the home is not the transferor's principal residence or cannot become the inheritor's within a year (which decides the post-transfer assessment), or if the proceeds would not actually be invested.

What would change the answer. Q01 (estate inclusion), Q02 (estate size and portability), Q05 (who lives in the home and when) and Q07 (the post-2027 Proposition 19 allowance) are the four facts that can reverse the ranking. They are listed first on the questions page, and four sensitivity runs on the decision page show the size of each effect.

The four facts that decide it

Q01 - Is the home in the gross estate?

Decides whether the basis resets. Without it, selling loses its advantage.

Q02 - How large is the whole estate?

Sets the room before federal estate tax and whether liquidity is needed at death.

Q03 - Will the trust be the owner, and how is it titled?

A trust is not a tax exemption; the interest held decides inclusion and basis.

Q05 - Who occupies the home at transfer?

Proposition 19 relief needs the home as the transferee's principal residence within one year.

Q07 - What will the Proposition 19 allowance be?

The official figure after 2027-02-16 is not published yet; the model must project it.

Q11 - What does insurance actually cost?

No binding quote exists for either property; every premium figure here is a placeholder.
