Decision readiness / 09

Questions before deciding

Eighteen answerable questions and five unknowables, each with what is held, what is missing, how to discover it, and who answers it.

A decision cannot be reasonable until the decisive questions have answers. This page separates three kinds of gap: facts that exist and must be collected (Q01-Q18), facts that exist but are only knowable near the event (Q07, future law), and facts that are unknowable by nature (U01-U05). Only the first kind blocks a decision. The unknowable ones are handled by testing many paths instead of assuming one.

The eighteen questions that can be answered

Each card states what is already held, exactly what is missing, and a concrete way to discover it. Where an answer is unknown it stays unknown: no value on this site was filled in by inference.

Q01 open

Is Property A in the transferor's gross estate, and will the inheritor get a basis reset?

Why it decides
Decisive. The basis reset is the entire case for selling. Without it the model shows selling falling behind renting.
What we hold
The transfer is by trust. The date of death is the assumed basis date.
What is missing
The trust document, every amendment, and whether the transferor keeps a retained interest or a power that pulls the home back into the estate.
How to discover it
A licensed estate attorney reads the trust and gives a written opinion. Secondary check: the executor's draft Form 706 shows the home as an includible asset.
Who answers
Estate attorney, then the executor
Official source
IRS-INHERIT-FAQ · IRS-ESTATE
Q02 open

How large is the whole estate, and was a deceased spouse's exclusion ported?

Why it decides
Decisive. It sets whether federal tax is due, and whether the estate must sell something to pay it.
What we hold
The house is modelled. The cash inheritance is modelled at six levels from a small amount to a large one.
What is missing
The value of every asset in the estate, the debts, and whether Form 706 was filed for a prior spouse.
How to discover it
The transferor's own asset schedule, prepared privately with the attorney. Do not upload it here. The prior Form 706, if any, is held by the executor or the preparer.
Who answers
Estate attorney and accountant
Official source
IRS-ESTATE
Q03 open

Will the trust be the owner on the title, and what interest does the inheritor actually receive?

Why it decides
Decisive for who can sell and when. A trust is not a tax exemption, and a beneficiary with a right to occupy is not the same as an outright owner.
What we hold
Nothing beyond the statement that the house passes by trust around 2040-2050.
What is missing
The vesting, the trustee's powers of sale, and whether the inheritor can compel a sale.
How to discover it
The deed and the trust. The county recorder has the recorded deed; the attorney reads the trust.
Who answers
Estate attorney; county recorder for the deed
Official source
IRS-ESTATE
Q04 open

Does the trust or the transferor hold the home in a way that changes the estate-tax result?

Why it decides
Changes the size of the estate, not the choice of path.
What we hold
Only that a trust is involved.
What is missing
Whether it is revocable, who the trustee is, and whether there is a retained life estate.
How to discover it
The trust document.
Who answers
Estate attorney
Official source
IRS-ESTATE
Q05 open

Who will occupy Property A at the transfer, and when?

Why it decides
Decisive for Proposition 19. The relief needs the home to become the transferee's principal residence within one year, and needs the exemption claim filed in that year.
What we hold
The plan is to decide whether to live in it. No occupancy decision has been made.
What is missing
Whether the inheritor will actually move in, and whether the timing works with living in the third unit of Property B.
How to discover it
A dated occupancy plan, written down. The assessor requires the exemption claim and can verify occupancy.
Who answers
The inheritor, then the county assessor
Official source
RTC-63.2 · BOE-HOX
Q06 open

Is Property A the transferor's principal residence, and is the land within the family-home definition?

Why it decides
A condition of the exclusion. Excess land is not part of the family home and is reassessed.
What we hold
The property is described as a house in the same ZIP code as the valuation.
What is missing
Whether the transferor uses it as a principal residence, and how much land is more than the homesite.
How to discover it
The transferor's exemption history and the assessor's parcel map.
Who answers
County assessor, with the attorney
Official source
BOE-LTA-2026-026
Q07 open

What will the Proposition 19 allowance be for a transfer after 16 February 2027?

Why it decides
Sets the post-transfer assessed value if the home is lived in. A larger allowance means a lower tax for living in it.
What we hold
The published allowance of $1,044,586 for transfers through 15 February 2027.
What is missing
The February 2027 figure. It is not published yet.
How to discover it
The BOE publishes a letter to assessors each time the allowance is re-indexed, every second February 16. Check the letter for the year of the transfer.
Who answers
BOE publication; county assessor
Official source
BOE-LTA-2025-009
Q08 open

What is Property A actually worth on the date of transfer?

Why it decides
Sets the assessed value, the sale proceeds and the headroom.
What we hold
A current estimate of about 3,100,000, and an assumed growth rate.
What is missing
A date-of-death appraisal. The model uses an estimate and a growth assumption, which is the largest single uncertainty in the projection.
How to discover it
A licensed appraisal at the time of transfer. Until then, the projection is a scenario.
Who answers
A licensed appraiser
Official source
FHFA-SJ
Q09 open

What is the current assessed value and base year of Property A, and is it accurate?

Why it decides
Sets the "prior value" in the Proposition 19 formula.
What we hold
The owner supplied a year-by-year history. That history is not published here because the pattern identifies the parcel.
What is missing
A current bill with the base year, and confirmation that the roll value is correct.
How to discover it
The current bill, and an informal review request to the assessor if the value looks wrong.
Who answers
County assessor
Official source
SCC-RATES
Q10 open

Is Property B correctly described as three livable units, and is the third unit legal?

Why it decides
Sets the rent, the rent-control coverage and the insurance product.
What we hold
Three livable units; two let; one occupied by the inheritor. The monthly rent for the two let units is known.
What is missing
The legal unit count, the certificate of occupancy, and whether the third unit can be let.
How to discover it
The planning department's property information, the certificate of occupancy and the rent board's unit registration.
Who answers
City planning department; rent board
Official source
SF-RENT-LAW
Q11 open

What does insurance actually cost for each property and occupancy?

Why it decides
A large, unverified line in every projection. It also decides what a loss would cost.
What we hold
The owner's estimate, rounded to $10. No quote.
What is missing
Two written quotes for Property A (homeowners, then landlord) and one for Property B as a three-unit building with one owner-occupied unit.
How to discover it
A broker or several direct insurers, in writing. The state publishes a premium comparison tool and a residential insurance guide.
Who answers
Insurance broker
Official source
CDI-RES
Q12 open

What is the solar agreement, and how long does the credit last?

Why it decides
The electricity line is modelled as a credit for fifty years, which is optimistic if the tariff period is shorter.
What we hold
A solar system is installed and the property generates a small net credit.
What is missing
The interconnection date, the tariff (NEM 1.0, NEM 2.0 or the net billing tariff), and the remaining term.
How to discover it
The interconnection agreement and the utility bill. The utility can confirm the tariff.
Who answers
The transferor; the utility
Official source
CPUC-NEM
Q13 open

What are the real water and electricity costs for each property?

Why it decides
Small in dollars, but the owner's figures are bills, not quotes, and they are the only basis.
What we hold
Monthly ranges from the owner.
What is missing
Twelve months of bills, and the water system that serves the address (three systems serve the city).
How to discover it
The bills, or the utility's own rate schedule for the address. The city publishes which system serves each area.
Who answers
The transferor and the utility
Official source
CUP-WATER
Q14 open

What repairs does Property A need now, and what will it need over the holding period?

Why it decides
Sets whether the house can be sold as-is and what the maintenance allowance should be.
What we hold
An annual allowance, not an inspection.
What is missing
An inspection report and a contractor's estimate for deferred work.
How to discover it
A pre-sale inspection, obtained privately. A roof, foundation or electrical defect changes the sale price.
Who answers
A licensed inspector or contractor
Official source
CDI-RES
Q15 open

What rent can Property A actually achieve?

Why it decides
Renting is only competitive if the rent is much higher than the HUD benchmark suggests.
What we hold
The HUD small-area fair-market rent for a three-bedroom, which is a 40th-percentile benchmark and not a valuation of this house.
What is missing
A local rental appraisal, and the condition the house would need to command it.
How to discover it
Two letting agents' written opinions, or recent comparable lettings.
Who answers
A local letting agent
Official source
HUD-SAFMR
Q16 open

What will the inheritance actually cost in fees?

Why it decides
Reduces the cash available, and is missing from the model.
What we hold
Nothing. The model does not carry legal, trustee or appraisal fees.
What is missing
A fee schedule from the attorney and the trustee.
How to discover it
Ask for a written estimate of probate-avoidance work, trust administration and appraisal fees.
Who answers
Estate attorney and trustee
Official source
PROB-16061.7
Q17 open

Will the inheritor be a California resident at the time of transfer?

Why it decides
Residency affects state income tax on the sale and the language of the trust documents.
What we hold
Not known. The model assumes California treatment.
What is missing
Residency status at the transfer date.
How to discover it
A direct question for the attorney, answered in writing before the transfer.
Who answers
Estate attorney; tax preparer
Official source
IRS-TC409
Q18 open

What happens to Property B's rent if the inheritor moves out of the third unit or lets it?

Why it decides
The model leaves Property B unchanged. That is an assumption, not a known fact.
What we hold
The two let units and the owner-occupied third unit.
What is missing
Whether the third unit will be let, when, and at what rent under the rent ordinance.
How to discover it
A decision by the inheritor, and a rent board registration for the unit if it is let.
Who answers
The inheritor; rent board
Official source
SF-RENT
U01 unknowable

Unknown by nature: the growth rate of the two housing markets over the next fifty years

Why it decides
The dominant uncertainty. No source can resolve it.
What we hold
Fifty years of real history, and named crisis paths.
What is missing
Anything about the future.
How to discover it
Not discoverable. The right response is to test the decision against many paths, which the black-swan page does.
Who answers
No one
Official source

U02 unknowable

Unknown by nature: inflation over the next fifty years

Why it decides
Drives the property-tax cap, the cost lines and the real value of every result.
What we hold
A long-run average and the 1976-2025 distribution.
What is missing
Anything about the future.
How to discover it
Not discoverable. The model uses a baseline and replays history.
Who answers
No one

BLS-CPI
U03 unknowable

Unknown by nature: investment returns over the next fifty years

Why it decides
The sale case is a bet on a diversified fund. Its return is not knowable.
What we hold
An academic long-run average and an academic return series.
What is missing
Anything about the future.
How to discover it
Not discoverable. The break-even table shows the return at which renting would win instead.
Who answers
No one

U04 unknowable

Unknown by nature: future federal and state tax law

Why it decides
The estate exclusion and the capital-gains rates are statutory, not constitutional.
What we hold
The law in force on 13 November 2026, and the enacted schedule to 2027.
What is missing
Any future change.
How to discover it
Not discoverable. Re-check annually against the IRS and the Legislature; the watch list is on the law page.
Who answers
No one
Official source
IRS-WHATS-NEW
U05 unknowable

Unknown by nature: the exact date of the transfer

Why it decides
The model uses a single date. The stated window is a decade wide.
What we hold
A window of 2040 to 2050 and a baseline of 2045.
What is missing
The actual date.
How to discover it
Not discoverable, and not needed: the decision analysis can be repeated for each year in the window, and the ranking does not change within it.
Who answers
No one
Official source
IRS-ESTATE
