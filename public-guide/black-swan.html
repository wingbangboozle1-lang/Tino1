Black swan and regime risk / 04

When the baseline breaks

Growth and inflation are never constant for fifty years. This page replays the real record instead of averaging it, and names the crises that change the answer.

Why a separate section. The baseline projection compounds one growth rate and one inflation rate for fifty years. The real record does not look like that: from 1976 to 2025 the same economy produced inflation of 13% and deflation, house-price years of +29% and -15%, and stock-market years of +52% and -37%. A decision that only works on the average is not a decision, it is a bet on the average.

What the stress tests actually say

Selling survives every twenty-year window

Replaying the real record from each of its 50 starting years, selling leads after twenty years in all 50 and after ten years in 43. The median ten-year advantage is $3,317,670; the worst is -$2,068,710. That is the risk being accepted: a bad decade is possible, a bad twenty years is not in the sample.

Two paths invert the answer

If the next fifty years look like Japan after 1990, or like the Depression, renting wins. Both are long-period, low-return environments where a house that produces rent beats a portfolio that does not. The practical response is not to reject selling; it is to invest the proceeds in stages and keep a few years of spending in short-term Treasury bills, so that a fall in the first years does not force a sale at the bottom.

The right response to a black swan

  1. Do not rely on one sale date

    Invest in tranches over twelve to twenty-four months rather than all at once. The model is a single-date calculation; the real plan does not have to be.

  2. Keep a cash and bill reserve

    The replays show the damage in a bad decade comes from being forced to sell investments to live. A reserve removes that forcing.

  3. Insure the risks that are insurable

    Earthquake is excluded from a standard California homeowners policy. If the house is kept, the uninsured loss is a real black swan; if it is sold, it is transferred with the house.

  4. Re-check after a regime change

    If inflation runs above 4% for several years, or house prices fall more than 20%, the ranking should be recomputed rather than assumed.

What a black-swan section cannot do. It cannot give a probability to a named crisis. The figures below are conditional outcomes - "if this happened, this is what the three paths would produce" - and they are labelled by provenance so that a government statistic is never confused with a construction. The 1976-2025 replays are the closest thing here to evidence, and they are 50 observations from one history.
